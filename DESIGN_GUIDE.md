# Venus University — Design Guide

## 0. How to read this guide

- This is the jumping-off point for anybody, human or agent, changing this repository: what the game is, where things live, and the rules a change has to keep. Read it before the code, and read `UI_STYLE_GUIDE.md` as well for anything that shows on screen.
- The code is authoritative. Where this guide and the code disagree, the code is right and the guide is stale: fix the guide.
- Present tense, nothing aspirational: a feature not in the code does not exist, whatever is written here.
- Appearance — shape, colour, type, layout, motion and the class names that carry them — is `UI_STYLE_GUIDE.md`'s and is never restated here.
- Hand-authored and disk-derived inventories are not listed here: backgrounds, poses, tag vocabularies, the class and shop catalogs, the lorebook, the bots' scripts. Only the rules they follow are.

## 1. What the game is

- A local, single-player, AI-driven dating sim in visual-novel form: an Electron desktop app for Windows with an NVIDIA or an AMD (RDNA 3 or 4) GPU. React 19, TypeScript (strict), Zustand, Motion (`motion/react`), Vitest, plain CSS. No server, no accounts, no env vars — everything the player configures lives in `data/settings.json`.
- **The writer is a cloud LLM.** By default it is Google Gemini over the native API; the player may instead name any endpoint that speaks OpenAI chat completions, with its own key, model id and reasoning effort. It invents characters, writes every scene as structured JSON the app plays back VN-style, answers two cheap classifiers, and keeps the books between scenes. The persona it writes as is RITA. The cloud pictures — rooms, the graduation picture — are always Gemini's image model, drawn with the Gemini key under either provider and skipped without one.
- **The artist is a locally managed ComfyUI**, installed under `data/services` by the app itself. It is optional: creating characters needs it, playing does not, and in a game it draws only the photos characters text.
- **The premise is fixed**: the reader is a freshman at Venus University in the city of Veridan for one semester, January 19 (a Monday, day 0) to the Friday nearest May 21. Two slots a day, day and night; a slot holds at most one scene. He may start it with one ex, a girl of the roster New Game picks, written by the game as a freshman who dated him before day 0. He may also pick a course from a closed list of fifteen written by us (`shared/courses.ts`, append-only, the exact name stored on the playthrough record and the enrollment, absent where he picks none and on Quickstart); the `READER` block says it as one sentence and nothing else changes without one. The courses the roster studies stay free text.
- **The end is reachable, not winnable.** Graduation morning opens an authored epilogue of goodbyes. Two things end a playthrough early: debt past a floor, and being caught at something expellable.
- **Content**: scenes may be explicit. Two settings shape it — `noNsfwImages` withholds the nude wardrobe, the CGs and the sexy and nude photos, `lessNsfwText` softens what RITA is asked for. A man has neither the nude wardrobe nor the CGs whatever the setting, while `MEN_ADULT_ART` in `shared/characterRules.ts` is off: never offered, never rendered — the run skips such a bucket whoever asked — and never ready on the stage, so a scene substitutes as the setting does. Neither is a filter, and the notes beside them are the whole of what the app promises.

### Vocabulary that is load-bearing

- **The reader** is the protagonist in the fiction ("he"); **the player** is the person at the keyboard. Prompts and copy say "the reader".
- **Memories** are what one character recalls about the reader. **History** is the playthrough log of finished-scene summaries; the **encounters** kept beside it say, for each finished scene with anybody in it, who was there, where, and which of them took in its summary. The **transcript** is the scene as delivered — the reader's action lines and every reply line — and the writer is shown its tail; the **scene log** is everything the player has read. None of them is "chat history". A **fact** is something lasting a finished scene showed about a character or the reader — her family, her past, a habit, a promise, a secret — kept as a phrase, either known to have happened or only said, with the characters who know it.
- A **playthrough** is one continuous game: a folder of saves plus the one **record** (`playthrough.json`) they are all read against; before the record exists the folder holds one **enrollment** instead, the semester waiting on its timetable. A **save** is a file in it — a slot-save at a boundary, one **autosave**, the last decision point reached, or one of ninety **manual saves** the player writes from the menu.
- An **event** is a plan the reader agreed to with somebody. An **occasion** happens whether he turns up or not, belongs to nobody, and is the only one of the two that can close the university.
- **charId** is the on-disk identity (a UUID). **charKey** (`first_last`) is the prompt identity — the only form the model sees or emits — translated at the edge and never used in a path. Neither is the display name.
- The **roster** is everybody in the playthrough — twelve to sixteen characters, the number picked at New Game (`shared/roster.ts`) and never fewer than twelve; the **cast** is who is in the scene. A **slot** is one half of one day; the **boundary** is the moment a slot ends and the next begins.
- A **group** is a team (fraternities and sororities follow): members drawn from the roster, a place and a fixed weekly agenda; the table is code (`shared/groups.ts`) and who belongs is settled once at New Game. A group's events are occasions with a place, dated at New Game around the fixed and generated ones.
- The **shipped cast** lives under `assets/characters` and is read-only; the **player's own** characters live under `data/characters`.

## 2. Design and mechanics

**Time.** The fixed occasions — holidays, add/drop day, midterms and finals weeks, spring break, summer vacation, graduation — are code. Each save invents a handful more at New Game (a gala, a dance, a career fair, a concert, weekly fillers); a generated occasion can never cancel a class. An occasion announces itself ahead in the prompts — a half-day the slot before, a day the day before, a run as many days before as it lasts — except those with a lead-up of their own. Weekends hold no classes. Spring break sends the half of the roster the reader likes most off campus for the week; summer keeps everybody on campus until graduation. The semester has two seasons read off the date, winter through March 15 and spring from the 16th (`src/shared/season.ts`), and the scene's, the phone's and the slot opening's prompts say which one in their lines below the cached prefix; the draw of the weather never changes it.

**Weather.** The whole semester's sky is rolled once at New Game and written on the playthrough record, one reading per slot: clear, rain or a thunderstorm. Rain tends to keep falling for a few half-days, a storm mostly grows out of rain and then clears the air, and storms lean toward the evening and grow commoner as the term runs on; the authored first day, graduation, the Selkie MusicFest and the holidays held outdoors are clear whatever the roll said, and the epilogue's night is always clear. The prompts are told what the sky is doing and for how long it has been doing it. A wet slot draws a background's rain render where one is shipped and the dry one otherwise, plays the rain or storm loop in place of the room tone — heard indoors through a window, so a silent room is not silent under rain, and the landing, where he is indoors on his phone, hears the same window — and wears the weather's mark where the sun or the moon would be: on the splash, the scene stamp, the landing and the calendar, which also forecasts the week ahead. The sky is another thing than the season (a warm day in February is still winter), and it does not snow in the valley at this stage: the world text says so, and that sentence goes when snow arrives.

**Characters.** A character is a man or a woman and is attracted to one of the two, both picked in the New Character modal and changeable in the editor; the reader is always a man. A character is written by the LLM from a short brief and that pick: one woven personality paragraph, five behavior strings (one per relationship stage), a backstory, a four-field love life, likes and dislikes, gift preferences, a closed set of trait switches, the one reader stat she goes for, the pitch of her voice, which the player retunes, booru appearance tags that open on the subject tags every render needs — `1girl` and `mature_female` for a woman, `1boy`, `male_focus`, `mature_male` and `handsome` for a man, swapped when the editor changes the gender — hers to edit like the rest and left out of the writer's prompts, one pose, and a seed. Every image she has comes from ComfyUI under that seed, the photos she texts aside, each under a seed of its own: the expression sprites, and optionally the alternate wardrobes, the CGs and a day/night room. Her portrait is cut from the neutral sprite, which always looks at the viewer whatever eye tag she was written with. A regenerate opens on the prompt it is about to send — every tag as an editable list and the seed it would run under, a new random one by default; one expression is re-rendered on its own by clicking its sprite, as one CG is by clicking it in the gallery. Each of those buttons keeps every group it last sent, whole, on her record and opens on them rather than on her tags from then on, so an edit to her tags reaches it only through the modal's reset, which puts back the groups her record would send and writes nothing until the render goes; the seed is never kept. A text's photo is the exception: its regenerate keeps its groups on the message and writes nothing to her record (The Bunnyboard). A fill, and a button that has sent nothing, render from her tags. Which images exist is read off disk, never recorded, and a partial set counts as absent. Beside the three stock wardrobes she has five custom slots, each a wardrobe rendered from tags the player types and named by him: the slot's Outfit group *is* her record, written before every confirmed render and read back from there when its modal opens, whatever its button kept; a slot is emptied images first and record second, what its button and its sprites' buttons kept going with it; RITA is never offered a custom set and the missing-content sweep never invents one, so the Cast modal's lock is its only way onto the stage. A sixth set, her winter one, is the code's: rendered from tags the player types in the editor, it stands in place of her usual clothes from January 19 to March 15 once all seven sprites exist, and `wearsWinter` (`shared/season.ts`) is the one rule the stage, the scene's `Main Outfit:` line and her phone photos ask — tags alone never put it on her. RITA is never offered it, the Cast modal's Default lock shows it, the missing-content sweep plans it only for a character whose record carries its tags, and a sprite saved in a scene stays the bare one, the swap being made when it is drawn. Traits are switches the code branches on and are never written into a prompt. The brief she was written from, reference picture included, sits on her record and beside it in her folder until her sheet lands, so a write that failed or was interrupted is resumed from her card in one click; a card whose sheet was never written opens nothing. The New Character modal's Skip LLM filling box writes her sheet on the spot instead — her names, the description as her personality, the first pose in the manifest her gender may stand in, one stock tag set per expression, `body` as her stat and nothing else — with no brief kept and nothing rendered, so her card opens at once on an empty sheet the player fills and renders by hand. The modal's suggested names and description are written for women, so a man is offered none: a blank field is left to the LLM, and a man with Skip LLM filling waits on both names being typed. A written sheet with an empty field is a failed write, except that a character who cannot be the reader's romance may leave the kinks and the crush and lover behaviors empty. The generation request is written for a woman attracted to men and says otherwise when the character is not one; a man is asked for his appearance and his outfit's skin exposure from his own tag lists, and his draft keeps only a man's categories. The image model leans to women, so every render of a man — sprite, expression and CG — adds a fixed set of women's tags to its negative, at render time and never on the record. A man stands only in the poses the manifest marks for men: the request offers him those alone, a pick outside them falls back to the first of them, and the editor lists them after whatever pose he already has. A man starts at the tallest stage height, anyone else by her breast tag. Everything written about one character — prompts, memories as they are filed, system texts and on-screen labels — is in that character's own pronouns, and every profile the writer is shown opens with the gender outright, since the subject tags never reach it; an instruction about the cast at large, or about a character nobody can name, says "they". Memories already on a save keep the words they were filed in. A character's day carries a mood off a thirty-day menstrual cycle that also keeps her in on her period and makes a Promiscuous girl lustful around ovulation; a man has no cycle, so none of that, and no mood on the cycle's own days. Her pose, like any character's, is picked under Generation settings.

**The slot cycle.** A slot opens on a narration call (the first day is authored) behind a curtain, then the landing: the date, money, stats, the hour's commitments as buttons, topped up with the reader's lowest stats, and a text box. The first action is classified — who is named, class or work or project, public or private, where — the cast is drawn, and the scene call streams lines the player clicks through. Further actions are continuations on a rolling summary until RITA ends the scene. The ending fires its calls in parallel — the scene's ledger, the texting ledger, the goodbye and, where anybody is cast, the facts call — and writes one save when all the replies are in, the facts reply held on the scene beside the ledger's, none when the boundary ahead loses the game; the boundary applies the ledger, moves the clock and writes the slot-save. Everything the ending computes is projected and banked; the boundary assigns and never rolls, so a replayed boundary lands the same values. The boundary also files the scene under its slot: its summary in the history and, where anybody was cast, an encounter — the whole cast, whoever left before the end, the place the classifier gave or none, and the part of the cast that took in the summary, which is whoever was still on the stage at the end and was not left out of a kiss or a night the ledger reported as private. The summary and the stood-up plans are kept apart in the encounter and the history's line is the two rejoined by one function; a replayed boundary replaces the slot's encounter, and a solo scene, an exam or a goodbye files none. Once the boundary's curtain is down and before it says anything, the player is asked what each girl in the cast should remember of the scene — the ledger's memories of her, each to reword, retype or blank, and a blank row where it gave her none, then the facts the scene showed and the characters who came to know one already kept, each fact to correct or clear, each box to tick — and the answer is filed before the clock moves, so the slot-save carries it and a reload before it asks again; a boundary that ends the game, or one with nobody cast, asks nothing. While a reply is pending, still streaming or parked on the scene's ending, the player may interject: the unread lines are deleted, the departure clocks are charged as at any decision point, and an autosave is written before the continuation call goes out. An interjection taken from a rewound line cuts everything after that line, later turns included, and a gift whose turn was cut goes back into the inventory and a Breakthrough whose turn was cut is cut with it. The row stands over the whole reply, its last line included, until that line is turned; Go from the action box opened over the last line reaches the decision point exactly as a click would, and sends the words as the turn. It is offered nowhere in a solo scene, the orientation scene's first reply, an exam or the ending's status lines, and under a cover; a class scene's first turn locks it instead. A rewind moves back one already-read line, through earlier replies as far as the scene's first reply, never onto the reader's own line, and changes only what is shown; it is closed while a reply's first line has not yet shown; the lines it stepped back may be read forward again, each landing as a cut, the reader's own line passed over without being shown, never into a line not yet read; the decision point is re-reached that way only when the rewind was taken from it. Any reply line of the scene may be rewritten from the chat log — never the reader's own, a status line or the landing's narration before his first action: the rewrite lands on the log, on the transcript line it was delivered as, on the box when it is the line on screen and on the decision point held in memory, so leaving carries it; a rewrite of a line the running summary already covers drops the summary marks past it, so the next call carries the line verbatim; it writes nothing itself. While the scene's ending is under way a line may still be rewritten for as long as the ending can be interrupted, behind a warning Settings can turn off: the ending's banked save and bookkeeping keep the original words, and the summary marks are kept too, dropped only if the player then interjects — so a finished ending files the whole recap and an interjection carries the rewrite verbatim. Interjecting on a reply that ended the scene, or on the goodbye lines after it, warns first and then discards the ending's bookkeeping for the later ending to redo, the texting ledger excepted, which the next ending claims.

**Casting.** The classifier only names; the app decides who is in the room. Every refusal is composed locally — not enrolled, no job, nobody named is free, a name he has not learned. An unnamed public action draws from the free pool, favouring the unmet; a passerby may walk in; whoever is standing at the scene's location joins; the worker on shift is there whether or not named. The cast is fixed for the whole scene. A `hide:` starts a departure clock, and after a grace period off the stage she has left and the model cannot write her back. A CG is offered only while the stage as written holds one girl with a CG set on it, or nobody yet, and it ends the moment anybody else is shown or she herself leaves, by the scene or by the player's hand.

**Relationships are memory, not score.** Nothing about how she feels is a number on disk. Each memory is typed (loved, liked, disliked, hated) and dated with the day and the half of the day it was filed on, stamped by the one function every writer files through, the ex's seed below being the one exception; affection is derived from them with a recency weight and bucketed into disposition tiers, which pick the behavior string she is written with — a girl he has broken up with is an enemy only while her affection is at or below zero and at least a friend above it, a lover or a crush ahead of both, and one of them in the neutral tier is told as on good terms above zero and as resenting him at or below it, her kisses read as something over rather than as a start. A second reading, the Breakthrough meter, is derived from the same memories — every list she keeps, folded as every reader reads them — for a contact whose name he knows: each half of a day adds up its memories on weights and a cap of its own, not affection's, and the halves are summed oldest first inside a floor and a ceiling. A memory filed before the halves were kept has no half and counts for nothing, so a save from then opens with the meter empty; like affection it is calculated and never stored. The reader spends a full meter from a button beside the scene's box, offered for anybody standing in the scene whose name he knows and whose meter is full, from the first turn the cast is set — the second action of an ordinary scene, the first one he types in a hangout — and never on the phone, in an exam, in a solo scene, on the orientation or the goodbyes, nor while a reply is coming. It asks which of them where more than one is full, and for a direction, which is required; the box's words stay the turn's action. The use is held on the scene, tied to the line it was made on, once per character per scene, and is cut with that line by an interjection from a rewound line before it, a rewind alone cutting nothing; a failed turn retries it, and cancelling the turn takes it back and returns the words to the box. It empties her meter at once, in the scene, and the boundary files it on her once, as the point after which only later halves count. When a use was made the scene's ledger judges it too: it is told who, what was asked and the reader's words on the action it was made on, and answers for each with how it went — one closed list, worked, partly or failed, which the prompt and the schema both read — and a sentence of what happened. The code decides the memory's verb from the result, loved, liked or disliked, files it with a mark that sets it apart, and puts the same result on the use. A result for somebody who made no use, off the list or repeated is dropped with a warning, one with no sentence keeps its result and files no memory, and a use the reply says nothing about is left with neither. The marked memory goes through the memory question like any other and keeps its mark and half of the day whatever is rewritten; it counts for her affection, opens neither the crush roll nor the scene's bonded list, and moves nothing else the scene decides. It is of the use's own half, so her meter stays empty. The development build's F9 can fill a meter with ordinary memories, one for each of the last five halves of a day, and forget a character's uses. A memory is kept and prompted in the third person, "the reader" every time: the ledgers are asked for it that way, what they return is filed that way, one the player types is filed that way, and every memory the app writes itself is worded that way. Wherever the player reads one it is turned into the second person with the verb agreeing; an older save's second-person memory is shown as it is and turned into the third person for the prompts. From her contact page he may rewrite a memory's verb and words, never its day, half of the day or mark, and the rewrite reaches every copy she holds. Milestones — met, kissed, slept together, lovers, broke up, friendzoned either way, sharing, contact info, blocked — are flags the ledger's *events* transition, except the two intimate ones, which it reports as acts and the app derives from them; the model is asked for events, never for flags. A crush is rolled once, at the scene ending, gated on the reader meeting her preferred stat. Only a character who can be the reader's romance has any of it: a woman attracted to men, and never a man while `MEN_ROMANCE_READER` in `shared/characterRules.ts` is off — the one switch every romantic mechanic with the reader reads through `romancesReader`. Anybody else never rolls, is told to the writer as no romantic option for him, has every romantic milestone the ledgers report dropped and is taken out of every kiss and night they name, while contact and unblocking still land; romance an older save filed on them is cleared without a word at the next scene, their memories kept. Their preferred stat is what earns their respect instead, said to the writer and in the status hint, and nothing numeric. What her contact page reveals opens by tier and never closes again.

**Facts.** A scene's facts come from a call of its own, fired beside the ledger at the scene's end so that the ledger's schema and prompt never carry them. Each is about one person of the scene whose name the reader knows, or about the reader, in a category off one closed list that the prompt and the schema both read, as a phrase completing "<Name> …" in the voice memories are kept in: "the reader" every time. It is either something that happened or only something somebody said — the subject being who said it — and a fact the reply does not plainly mark as having happened is filed as only said. The reply is held to the scene before the player sees any of it: a fact about nobody the save holds, about somebody not in the scene or whose name he does not know, in a category off the list, with no words, or repeating one already kept is dropped with a warning, and no more than four a scene are kept. Who knows is the person it is about and whoever the reply named who was in the scene; with nobody named, only the subject, or for a fact about the reader whoever took in the summary; the reader always knows. A fact spreads only when a scene tells it: the call is shown, with ids, the facts somebody in the scene already knows, and says who of the scene came to know one; a learned row for somebody out of the scene, who knew it already, or for a fact nobody there knew is dropped. The call is never asked about what the game decides — where somebody lives, their year, major, job or classes, who is dating whom, an ex, a milestone — and a plan or promise is only remembered text that never reaches the calendar. Nothing proposed is read by the next slot's opening or projected by the ledger's projection: the facts are asked about at the boundary, and what is left in is filed there under ids of the slot and their place in the scene's list, so a boundary run twice files each once. A character's scene block lists, after her encounters, the facts she knows, newest within a budget by the size of the scene — thirty alone with the reader, twenty with two, ten with three or more — and the conversation on the phone twenty; a fact only said is told as her hearing it said, never as true, and a secret is told with the people in the scene who do not know it named by the code as those it must never be let show in front of. The call re-sends itself silently three times, spends one content block on the ledger's shortened resend with the scene swapped for its summary, and never asks the player: where it fails for good the scene is left without facts, with a warning in the console, and a Retry the player presses on the ledger does not send it again. A scene with nobody cast, an exam, a solo scene and the goodbyes make no such call. The development build's F9 says how many facts each character knows and how many are about her, and can file a test fact for a character, let a second one know the newest, file a run of filler facts and forget them all. A save without facts reads as none.

**The contact page's record.** A contact whose name he knows lists, under her memories, the facts she knows and the scenes she was in, newest first, each row opening its own edit. A fact he may correct — its words, filed as a scene's are, its category and whether it happened or was only said — delete for everybody, or take from her alone, which is not offered for a fact about her and deletes the fact when she was the last who knew it; he may also write one about her or about the reader, known to her and the reader and nobody else, under an id with an `a` where a scene's has an `n`, so it never meets one a boundary files, and the screen says that what the game decides has no effect as a fact. A scene she was in he may rewrite the recap of, for everybody who was there and for the calendar: the history line of that slot is recomposed from the new summary and the stood-up lines the encounter keeps, which the calendar and the next opening read, while an opening the ending already asked for is not redone; opened and saved as it stood, a recap of any length is untouched. He may take her out of one, out of who was there and who took in the summary, the text and the history kept for the rest, and the entry goes when nobody is left; he cannot add an encounter, and a slot from before encounters has none to list. Every removal asks first, and nothing here adds a field to the save.

**The reader's ex.** New Game may pick one girl of the roster who can be his romance, on its setup step and never in Quickstart, with how it ended, warm or bitter, and who ended it; the choice rides the enrollment and the playthrough record, and a record that names nobody the playthrough holds, or has none, reads as no ex. The profile call is given her year as a fact and the validation forces it, so she is a freshman. When the first save is written the game lays her story onto her entry, once: the milestones, one by one on their own days through the fold a scene's ledger takes, a name he knows and a contact's flag, and memories of the relationship and of how it ended, all dated before day 0, the breakup in the summer before the semester and the dating one to two years back, none with a half of the day, so her Breakthrough meter starts empty; the kiss and night tallies are never touched, and the seed is the only writer that files a memory without a half of the day. Her sheet is never changed: in that playthrough every prompt and her contact page leave out her dating history and her virginity and tell one paragraph in their place — where and when they dated, that it was before college and ended the summer before, and how — which her block gives after her backstory, as outranking it, and the `READER` block gives after the exes line, and which carries no city, no length and no month, so the dates of the exes line stand alone. A day before day 0 is printed with its year wherever something remembered is dated. She is never the first person of the orientation scene, whose fixed text says the two have not met rather than that he knows nobody, and the first contact BunnyBot congratulates is not the one she already is. Each scene she is in logs which of her five behavior texts she is written with and her affection.

**The reader's stats and money.** Brain, Body and Heart are points on the save, spoken of and shown only as tiers. A stat moves only for something that happened in a scene: what the ledger says the hour exercised (more of it alone than with company), attending class, a grade coming back. Money is not a stat: a dollar balance the ledger prices each scene against, gifts spend and a job pays. It can go negative, and past the debt floor it ends the playthrough. The model is never shown the balance. The reader also has a bio: a few sentences the player writes at New Game (the full flow only) and may rewrite on his profile, carried into every prompt\'s `READER` block as one sentence that completes "The reader is". The save keeps lifetime tallies — money earned, kisses, nights, shifts worked — moved only by the same idempotent boundary passes that move everything else, and beside them the output tokens the writer has generated for this playthrough, thinking included, credited on every cloud text reply as it lands (retries and failed replies too, New Game's own generation calls carried over from the enrollment, and never a picture), so a replayed call is counted again because it was billed again; his GPA is never stored, being derived at read time from the scores frozen and the meetings recorded so far, and standing at 4.0 before either exists.

**Academics.** The player picks lecture classes and a PE class, between a floor and a cap, from a catalog generated for the save; every roster schedule is dealt locally. The generated semester is written to disk the moment the registrar offers it, so a playthrough can be continued from the registrar before its first save exists. Lecture classes are assessed by a midterm and a final built out of the factoids the ledger recorded week by week — a skipped meeting is a question answered wrong. Project classes are assessed at two showcases by sessions worked. A grade is the share his stats buy plus what his work buys of the rest, frozen when the assessment is sat. The reader's course rides the class-generation call below the cached prefix, which must offer it with its four classes under its exact name, any spelling of it that differs in case or spacing being written as the list's (a student's too); those four classes are never dropped from the registrar for want of a student, and any character may be seated in one as in a filler. Add/drop day lets the timetable change once; a drop is permanent.

**Jobs.** The reader may hold one part-time job from a board, on a shift week that can never overlap a class. A shift is worked by spending the slot on it; a missed one is a strike, and enough of them and he is let go; clean streaks raise the wage. What a worked shift pays in stats is the employer's own, rolled when the shift is cast and kept on the scene, so a reload pays the same one. Roster characters hold jobs too, as whereabouts only: she is never judged or paid.

**Whereabouts.** Every character has a hidden weekly schedule of haunts, dealt at New Game after classes and shifts, as many people to a place in a slot as its capacity allows, one unless the list of places says otherwise. What the deal could not place — a haunt, a job's shifts, a whole week — is written on the record beside her haunts with the reason, logged at New Game and shown by the development build's F9. Each haunt comes from one of the profile's menus — somewhere she has fun, somewhere she studies, an activity the profile call describes in her own phrase, a meal out — and beside them the weekly grocery run and the nights she stays in. The kind is stored beside the place: it is what the prompts say she is there for, and what the rule emptying a study haunt through a week nobody has anything to study for reads. A place may keep hours of its own — a club's nights, a restaurant's dinners and weekend brunches — and neither a haunt nor an outing is placed outside them; an employer's shifts are its building's hours. Each slot also rolls an overlay of who is with whom — friends pulled along, weekend outings — off the roster's own affinities: a stored affinity per pair, moved a step by every meeting the slot held and settled at every scene ending. Where a thing goes:

- Anything both processes read, every vocabulary, and every rule main and the renderer must both keep goes in `src/shared`; each of them calls it. Every word the app says about a figure lives beside that figure's arithmetic, so retuning one reaches every reader.
- A prompt builder is pure and lives in `prompts/`; what sends it lives in `main`.
- A screen is one file in `views/` with one stylesheet in `vu_styles/`; a control is promoted to `components/` on its second caller; a mark a second screen needs moves into a `views/*Icons.tsx` file.
- Loop logic goes in `stores/loop/` beside the concern it belongs to, the pure half in its own file with `rand` injectable and the impure edge beside it.
- Runtime data lives only under the folder `paths.ts` roots; nothing else hardcodes a location.

## 4. Architecture rules

- **The renderer touches nothing but the bridge.** Main is behind it and is the only process that touches disk, spawns a process or calls a network.
- **The window shows one page and opens no other.** A navigation the renderer starts is refused; a link leaves through main and only for an `https:` page; the renderer is sandboxed, with devtools in dev only.
- **Every channel is `invoke`/`handle` and answers `Result<T>`** — `{ ok, data }` or `{ ok: false, error }` — with errors built only by `appError`. Nothing throws across the bridge and nothing is swallowed. Adding a channel edits `preload/api.d.ts`, `preload/index.ts` and `main/ipc.ts`.
- **Progress rides fixed broadcast channels**, never per-job channel names: the renderer learns a job id only after the events have fired.
- **Character images reach the screen through one URL builder — `charimg://` — and backgrounds through the bundler.** The URL always names the PNG; the shipped cast is the same image under `.webp`, every reader accepts either extension and every writer makes a PNG. Which images a character has is read off her folder, never recorded. A URL can only show an image; what needs pixels — the wardrobe repairs, the hand fix, the graduation sheet — reads bytes over the bridge.
- **Sound is bytes over IPC into one Web Audio graph**, with one bus per slider. What plays continuously is derived from store state by one pure function, and what just happened by a second, with a reloaded save counting as nothing having happened. A missing file warns once and is silence.
- **The job queue is serial and global.** Failures retry with backoff only for transport errors; there is exactly one permanent-vs-retryable list, in `shared/errors.ts`, and both processes read it. `cancelGroup` resolves only once the running job has settled; loop calls register under three cancel groups — the scene calls, the ending's bookkeeping, and everything else — and leaving cancels all three, an interjection the first, an ending interrupt the first two; the replies to the reader's posts and comments, the post tagging him and the comments between the roster each go under a group of their own, cancelled with their opening's and at their deadline. No wait inside a job is unbounded; every continuation re-checks that its character is still wanted after each `await`.
- **Nothing in the UI waits for ComfyUI**: callers fire and forget or queue a job, and the first job pays the cold start. Main owns the server's runtime state and broadcasts it on one fixed channel; the renderer's store mirrors it and never infers it from a call's result. ComfyUI starts when the player asks for it in Manage Characters, after an install completes and when a render job needs it, and nowhere else — never at boot, and a game only through the first photo a character texts, never by being loaded. Manage Characters can stop it by hand, and a stopped server stays down until the player starts it or a render job does.
- **One store per domain, no god store.** Only stores talk to the bridge; components read state and call actions; state one screen alone needs stays in React.
- **The game loop is imperative module code**, `gameLoop.ts` calling `stores/loop/*` with imports running one way and back-edges late-bound through `loop/hooks.ts`; there is no import cycle in `stores/`. Everything held between calls is one `loopState` object; `runToken` identifies one stay in a game and is re-checked after every `await`. At most one live scene call is ever in flight. The flags the screen must watch — a resolved scene's pending ending, whether an ending's bookkeeping is still out, the status sequence, the boundary's memory question — live on the store beside `busy`/`streaming`.
- **Errors have three tiers and none is silent**: a job failure marks its card and offers retry; a recoverable app error is a modal the app continues past; a fatal one is a blocking screen with copyable diagnostics.
- **Every console line lands in `data/app.log`** from both processes: main writes its own as it prints, the renderer's are forwarded over one channel. A log that will not open costs nothing but the log. The Feedback modal hands the player a copy of it, so nothing may print a secret. Both sinks — the file, and the error a failed channel answers with — replace the install, data and home paths with placeholders, so no line and no modal names the machine.
- **On disk**, every JSON record carries a `schemaVersion`; a read refuses a wrong version or a missing required field and defaults nothing. The one exception is a text's photo: a field that does not read whole, the groups it keeps included, is shown as no photo and never redrawn, the save still loads, and the field is written back as it was found. The second is the photo a banked opening's invitation carries: one that does not read whole, or is of a kind no invitation carries, is no photo, and its invitation is filed without it. Two records carry an upgrade, applied on read by a pure function beside the read rule and written straight back where it was read from: the character record, from either of the two versions before this build's, a step at a time, with `updatedAt` untouched — the last step reads `1boy` in the appearance as a man, whose appearance then leads with `1boy` and `male_focus` and drops a woman's subject tags, anyone else as a woman, each attracted to the other gender, and an export's manifest from either version is read as this build's; and the settings, whose custom endpoint's model ids move out of Gemini's fields when the file names none of the endpoint's own, which a file this build wrote always does. Every other record, and every other version, is refused, with no migration path. Writes are atomic: a temp file renamed over the target, the rename retried for about a second when Windows refuses it because a scanner or the indexer holds the file it just saw change. Any id that becomes a path segment is validated first. An absent optional is omitted, never `null`. What New Game settles goes on the playthrough record, written once and replacing the enrollment it was picked on; what moves goes on the save. A save may carry its own picture inside it, so a backup and a restore carry it too.
- **The reader's picture is the one file under a playthrough the player supplies**; the photos characters text are files the game writes there itself (below). The renderer cuts it to the portrait's proportions and encodes a PNG; whichever build writes it sniffs the bytes first under one shared rule; it is kept beside the record like the graduation picture, goes with the playthrough when that is deleted, and travels in a backup, where the export sniffs it again.
- **A text's photo is a file under its playthrough's `photos` folder named for its message, its seed and a code of its prompts** — one file per version, so a save reaches the version it records whatever was drawn since — the id and the seed validated before they become a path and the code hashed off the prompts by one shared function, in main off the prompts it is sent; a reader takes only that exact name, and a name of another shape is never read. Its job carries the playthrough's id, so it lands in the right folder whichever game is open: it draws into `data/tmp` and moves the picture into place only while it is still wanted, the playthrough's folder is still there — never made again — and the bytes are a PNG, the temporary file going whatever fails. A photo asked for again waits on the job already queued. Deleting a playthrough cancels its photos and waits for them first; a restore does the same for every photo. Photos never travel in a backup: the message keeps the seed and the prompts, and an opened conversation redraws whatever file is missing. A photo file no save of its playthrough names is an orphan, and Settings, opened with no game loaded — where a photo that just arrived is in no save yet — counts the `.png` files of every `photos` folder that are, asks, and deletes them; it refuses while any photo is queued or drawing, checked again at each deletion, and refuses the whole sweep, deleting nothing, when a save of any playthrough does not read, while a photo field that does not read whole keeps every file of its message and a playthrough with no save is left alone. A screen holds a photo's state by playthrough, message and version, shows a job's result only while the playthrough it was asked for is still open, and drops every state and picture on the way out of a game.
- **A save is written only at a point the player can be put back onto**, and every reply that arrived is paid for by a write before playback resumes. The registrar is such a point before any save exists, which is what the enrollment is for. Leaving for the menu and quitting the app from the Escape menu both write the decision point first. The autosave is the last decision point reached and stands until the next one overwrites it; a boundary leaves it alone. An ending the boundary will lose to — the ledger's expulsion, or the balance past the floor once the scene's money has moved — is written nowhere: no autosave, and the manual save is refused over its goodbye and status lines, so a lost game leaves only the saves before it. An interjection writes the cut scene as a decision point before its call goes out; a rewind writes nothing. A scene ending is one write or none. A manual save is refused while a call it would lose is out — a reply, a classifier, an ending's bookkeeping, a slot opening, a text or its typed release, a failed turn parked on retry — and it is absent from the goodbye menu once the balance is past the debt floor. Where it can be taken, a manual save records the line on screen with the lines not yet read still queued, so a load lands on that line and reads on with no call; while the ending's status lines are on screen it records the ending's start, taken before any money moved, so a load replays the sequence and pays once; mid-paper it records the question the reader is on. The autosave for an exam is written once, when its questions land, and a reload sits the paper again from its first question; a manual save resumes at its own question instead. A manual save and the autosave carry a small picture of the stage a load of them opens on — for a reply still queued, as its first spoken line leaves it — drawn from the same images the stage shows and only when somebody is on it; the load list's background for a save without one is read off the same lines; a boundary save carries none, and the load list shows the landing's background for it. Every pass a boundary runs is idempotent, because the boundary is replayed whenever the save before it is loaded.
- A queued save write belongs to the stay that queued it and is dropped if that stay has been left; leaving, quitting and switching saves wait for every queued write.
- The load list is read as summaries; a save is read whole only when it is loaded, and the newest save of a playthrough, whichever kind, is what Continue opens and what the playthrough list shows.
- **What a screen shows is derived where it can be**: the displayed stage, the calendar's classes, the map's rows and the feed's check-ins are computed at read time from facts, never stored beside them.
- **Booru tags are lowercase with underscores** in every vocabulary and prompt, normalized at the source; weight syntax is preserved as written; nothing the model returns is re-checked against a vocabulary except the pose, which cannot render without a skeleton and which a man may take only from the poses marked for men.
- **The custom slots are outfit sets like any other, and so is the winter one.** Their ids, and the winter set's after them, are appended to the outfit vocabulary, so every gate, folder, URL, render, repair and lock takes them as it takes the stock sets; only what labels a set, reads its tags, offers it to RITA or sweeps for it tells the two apart. A record carries a seed flag only for a set it has rendered, and a set with no flag follows the main seed.
- **A character's record has one writer in the renderer**, serialized per character: every write reads her fresh at its turn, so a seed a bucket records and a name the player types cannot lose each other, and a Save from the editor keeps the fields the run owns.
- **Configuration is the settings file**, edited through the app. Settings writes a select, a checkbox or a slider the moment it changes; the typed fields — the endpoint URL, the model ids, the reply cap and the keys — are staged behind Save and gated on the way out. The two keys — the Gemini key, which writes under Gemini and draws the cloud pictures under either provider, and a custom endpoint's own key beside it — are encrypted at rest and never reach the renderer, which sees a presence flag for each. Switching providers moves neither key and changes no other setting: each provider keeps its own model, secondary model and effort, so one switched away and back finds them as they were; a custom endpoint's key is kept only for the origin it was typed for, so a key never follows the player to another host. A custom endpoint's URL is any well-formed `http:` or `https:` URL, saved as typed, the Test connection button being how the player learns whether it answers; a provider switched to Custom before its URL is typed is kept off a game by the writer-ready gate. The game writes a backup of everything it keeps — one zip, never a key — and restores one behind a confirmation: saves, settings and grab bags are replaced and the player's own characters merged in by id, once every image in the archive has passed the same content check an import does. The shipped cast is the game's: it never travels in a backup, and a backup that names one of its ids is restored around her, record and images alike, so a change to the cast is not written over by a backup made before it. A few dev switches are hand-edited and have no UI.
- **One run at a time.** A second launch brings the first window forward and exits before it prints, spawns or touches `data`; the packaged exe and the dev app share a user-data folder, so each stops the other.
- **The app comes up behind a cover already down.** Nothing is drawn until the stores are in and the first screen has been chosen under it; a fatal error drops the cover with the sequence it was raised for.
- **The first run is a sequence of stages on one screen** — the API key, the ComfyUI install, the content question — where a stage already settled is skipped and a player who quits part-way is put back at the first stage still unsettled. The key stage offers both providers — a Gemini key and model, or a custom endpoint's URL, model id and optional key with a Test connection — stages everything locally and writes nothing until Save; a custom endpoint's effort, reply cap and secondary model wait in Settings. The content question is last, and answering it is what ends the run.
- **Authored pools are dealt from grab bags**: the stranger's post, the inspiration word, the rumor place, the filler sentences. Half the pool drains before anything can repeat. The set-aside lists live in `data/grabbags.json`, shared by every playthrough, written behind the draw and flushed before the app quits. The stranger post's handle bag holds the generated names beside every supporter's, a supporter carrying as many marbles as their contribution earns on a diminishing scale, and the handle is drawn before the message: a marble cannot repeat before the bag turns, but a name with several of them can, and a line marked strangers-only is never dealt to a supporter's name. The gossip's handle comes from a bag of its own that holds the generated names alone; the anonymous replies to the reader's posts take theirs off the same names, picked by the post's id and outside any bag.

## 5. Prompts and the LLM

- **Builders are pure** and return `{ system, user, schema, cacheKey }`; main attaches the key and sends. Arrays are joined with newlines, so one element is one line the model reads — no sentence is ever broken across two. A builder never draws from a grab bag: the caller draws the inspiration word or the rumor place and passes it in.
- **Order is by rate of change, slowest first.** `system` is what never varies while a playthrough runs (persona, JSON rules, setting); `user` is everything else, cheapest-to-change last and the reader's action at the very end. Scene calls key their cache on the playthrough, never the save; one-shots key on a constant. A list that reaches a prompt is sorted, never shuffled, so the cached prefix holds. A `user` message that opens with a preamble fixed for its cacheKey marks where the preamble ends with `logFrom`, and the console log starts there; the system prompt is never logged.
- **The writer's window is the running summary plus the transcript's tail.** Every continuation and closing call sends the summary alongside the latest stretch of the transcript verbatim, taken as whole lines from the tail under a word budget and never shorter than the part the summary does not yet cover; the summary a reply arrives with covers the transcript up to the point that reply's prompt was built, and each summary is kept with the mark it covers up to, so a cut reverts to the right one. The ledger reads the whole scene.
- **What a scene knows of her past with the reader is her memories, her encounters and her facts.** After her memories a cast block lists the last scenes she was in, oldest first, as many as her memories' budget allows up to twelve — twelve with one person in the scene, ten with two, five with three or more — and the conversation on the phone lists her last five. Every entry gives the day, the half, the place and who else was there, a name the reader has not learned marked as unknown to him; only the newest half of the entries (rounded up) also carry the scene's summary, and only where the encounter says she took it in. The block says it is recaps for continuity and that she knows of those scenes only what her own entry says. A scene from before encounters was kept has no entry, so it is in no block. The ledgers, the feed's replies and the slot opening carry none; the facts call is shown the facts somebody in the scene already knows and no encounter.
- **Structured outputs everywhere**: the schema goes verbatim as `responseJsonSchema` to Gemini and as a non-strict `response_format` JSON schema to a custom endpoint; declared key order is load-bearing (`lines` before `summary`), and leaving a property out of the schema is the only reliable way to stop the model filling it. Gemini rejects a schema past a complexity cap with a bare `INVALID_ARGUMENT`, so a reply carrying a record per character is an array of records with a `key` enum and no item bounds.
- **Never trust a reply.** Every value is sanitized: an unknown key, background or action is dropped or defaulted with a console warning, never thrown on, because one bad field must not cost the player the scene. Streaming is a preview channel only; the resolved reply is sanitized whole and reconciled, and a preview that disagrees is discarded rather than spliced. The one carve-out is an interjection: lines the player has already read from a call he then interjects over are canon, and the call is aborted.
- **Prefetch, bank, apply.** The next slot's opening is fetched at the scene ending and banked on the save, the photo one of its invitations carries and the likes and replies of the reader's waiting posts, the replies to his waiting comments, the post tagging him and the roster's comments under the slot's posts settled and banked with it; a hangout scene is written the moment it is armed; the texting ledger is sent when the scene's cast is set and its reply banked on the scene, keyed on the message set it read and the feed exchanges that closed in the slot, so a reload claims it rather than paying again; an exam's chain when its questions land. An interrupted ending's banked and in-flight bookkeeping is dropped and re-sent by the later ending, the facts call's reply with it, except the texting ledger, which the next ending claims. Nothing an ending reads is applied, only projected; the boundary applies.
- **A request is built once and re-sent verbatim.** A retryable code spends a silent backoff budget first, except a depleted prepaid balance, which a resend cannot refill; a content block earns one silent shortened resend; a malformed reply is never auto-retried once the player has read its preview. What survives is a modal, and leaving it loses nothing, because nothing was written; editing the prompt by hand is offered only on a content block, since nothing else that failed was the prompt.
- **Four kinds of call may route to a cheaper secondary model** — the slot opening, which the epilogue's status updates ride, texting, the exam quiz and the solo scene; cast scenes, ledgers, the facts call, classifiers, the replies to the reader's posts and comments, the post tagging him, the comments between the roster and character generation never do. Both classifiers run at no less than low thinking and both ledgers and the facts call at high, whatever the setting says. A custom endpoint's effort is minimal until set, and the floors raise it like Gemini's.
- **Ask for events, not flags; for a boolean, not a magnitude.** The app holds every flag and applies every transition. The model is never shown the balance, never told the departure grace period, and never given a character's haunts. A judgment is not a flag either: the ledger says how a Breakthrough went from a closed list and the code alone decides what that files, so no event, milestone or flag comes out of it, and the field is asked for only on a scene that used one.
- **The ledgers are asked for memories in the reader's voice** — "the reader", never "you" — and every desc is put through the same rewrite again as it is injected, so an older save's second-person memory reaches the model the same way.
- **The classifier decides nothing.** It names, the app enforces, and every refusal is local; a transport failure parks the turn rather than degrading to a guess.
- **Every scene-kind fact is decided where the verdict is cast**, held on the store, persisted on the scene, and injected on every turn of it. The one exception is a Breakthrough: only the continuation of the action that used it carries its block, above the action and outside the scene so far, so a retry and a shortened resend keep it and no later turn does. It gives the direction the reader asked for and, where the game says she cannot be his romance, that the step forward is friendship, trust or respect; it bends the text the writer writes and never an event the app records.
- **The lorebook is keyword-triggered** on the turn's own words and on the place the classifier named; entries never cascade. An uncast character who is named gets a paragraph that says she is absent, what she looks like, and where she stands with the cast and the reader.
- **Authored text never meets the LLM or the sanitizer**: the first day, the bots, the game-over endings, the graduation scroll and every status line are the app's own writing.

## 6. Coding conventions

- TypeScript strict with `noImplicitAny` on explicitly. There is no linter; `npm run typecheck` and `npm test` are the automated checks.
- **Naming**: PascalCase only for React component and view files; camelCase for everything else. CSS classes are `vu-` prefixed and every visual rule lives in `vu_styles`.
- **Imports**: `@shared` is the only alias and is used for every cross-tree import of `src/shared`; imports inside a tree stay relative.
- **Comments say what the thing below them is or does.** One above every function, one or two lines, three where a mechanic genuinely needs it; a file's docblock at most five. Never what it isn't, never a rejected alternative, never history, and never a pointer to a guide or a section. A rule that needs explaining belongs in this guide, once.
- **Pure and impure halves are separate**: a roll takes `rand` as an argument, the impure edge reads and writes the store, and a test reaches the pure half.
- **A vocabulary is authoritative in code** and printed into the prompt and the schema from the same constant, so the two cannot drift; a new value is appended, never inserted, so cached prefixes stay byte-identical.
- **One definition per fact.** When two places must agree — how a charKey is derived, whether she is busy, whether a code is permanent, whether a schedule is complete, who is in a class — they call one function.
- **A control that is not the player's to use is absent; one that is dead says nothing.** A disable notice is an exception approved by name. Placeholders are real values a blank field submits, with one exception: an empty prose field of the character sheet shows the shape its text wants, told about her by name, and submits nothing.
- **A character whose name the reader has not learned is never shown by face or name** outside the scene she is in: showing the face is naming her, and a stranger is omitted rather than masked.
- **Nothing is shown as points.** A number on screen is a tier; the raw figure appears in one place, small, beside the chart.

## 7. Tests

- The suite defends **logic that can silently corrupt a save, and the pure functions a save is computed from**. A loud failure is found the first time anyone plays; a plausible-but-wrong save is invisible for hours. IPC wiring, the ComfyUI and setup services, and every view stay untested by design.
- **Never test** prompt wording or block order, formatting, labels or copy, or shipped content — a lint of a hand-written file restates the file.
- **Err on the side of fewer tests.** Creating none for a feature and verifying it by hand is a valid outcome. Anything neither the type checker nor the suite can catch is exercised in the running app before it is called done.
- The environment is `node`: no test touches the DOM or React. Stores are exercised through `setState`/`getState`. `test/fixtures.ts` holds every builder more than one file wants, and `stubApi`, which installs a partial `window.api` — anything not stubbed throws. A builder that reads a store stays in its own file: `fixtures.ts` imports no store, or a suite that mocks a module beneath one would load that store from inside its own mock factory.
- One file per unit under test; the file names are the index, and each `describe` says what it defends.

## 8. External services

- **ComfyUI** is installed, verified and run by the app under `data/services/comfyui`: a runtime release, its custom nodes and its models, with `shared/setupManifest.ts` authoritative for every tag, commit, URL, size and hash. The runtime is one of two pinned assets of that release, NVIDIA or AMD ROCm, chosen by the GPU vendors Electron reports and overridden by the hand-edited `comfyGpu`; both are launched with the same arguments, ComfyUI reading its backend off the torch it was built against, and a build that is not the one the machine resolves to fails verification rather than falling back to the CPU, the next install replacing it and everything under it. On the ROCm build alone the background-removal node's source is patched to run its model on the CPU, at install and again at every boot that finds the patch missing; the NVIDIA build's file is never touched. Nothing is fetched from a moving reference — the runtime comes from a named release tag and a node from a named commit — and no download is accepted that is not over https or does not match the manifest's size and SHA256; a node's Python dependencies come from the package index only, at the exact versions the manifest names, since a player has neither git nor a build chain. Every zip the app opens is unpacked by the bundled 7-Zip and only after its whole listing has been read and accepted: no entry may be absolute, leave the destination, repeat a name or be a link, and an archive past its entry-count or uncompressed-size cap is refused unopened. A character import and a backup restore hold to the same rule once the zip is open: besides the record, the marker and a staged run's images, every entry must be one of her images under either extension with its bytes sniffed rather than trusted, or the whole archive is refused before anything is written, and the export, duplicate and backup writers copy only what that gate accepts, so the app never writes an archive it would refuse. The workflows are API-format graphs executed as they are, every prompt string injected per job. One seed per character, the photos characters text aside; a regenerate renders into staging and commits by a directory swap, a fill writes in place. A single expression and a single CG render in place under a seed nobody records; a single expression is a face pass over the set's base frame, which is rendered only where the set has none. The app trusts only the server it owns: one already answering on the port is adopted only while a process is running from the app's own interpreter, an output path the server names is used only where it lands inside the output folder, and an image it answers is written only when its first bytes are a PNG's or a JPEG's. The development build's Manage Characters alone opens a photo lab, whose channels a packaged build refuses: it draws a portrait photo of any woman on the shelf, the shipped cast included, from tag groups it builds off her record, a kind, an outfit, a place, the half of the day and the weather, and shows for the player to rewrite; a box, ticked by default, sends it through the refining graph every photo in a game is drawn with; each render is queued under a group of its own and lands, with a text file of its prompts and seed beside it, in a folder under `data/tmp` that only the lab's own button empties, never in her folder.
- **The cloud LLM** is called by one adapter per wire format: Gemini's native `generateContent`, and OpenAI chat completions for the custom endpoint. The transport, the vendor table and the adapters are three files with three jobs; the table alone names a vendor for writing, and the custom entry's root is read off the settings at call time. The pictures always run on the Gemini adapter with the Gemini key, which the picture transport names outright, and the Settings form's two probes — the endpoint's model list and a one-line test call — go through the same transport. Every text call is capped at one shared output-token ceiling, whichever adapter sends it, except that a custom endpoint sends the player's own cap where one is set. What every text reply generated, thinking included, is read off the vendor's usage block by the transport on every exit — a blocked, truncated or malformed reply included — and reported through a port each process wires, reaching the renderer on one fixed broadcast channel before the reply itself does; the pictures report nothing. A cloud image is kept exactly as the model answered it, JPEG or PNG, under its `.png` name; the night re-light is the one reader that needs the type and sniffs it off the bytes.
- **Release.** `npm run release` builds the Windows app folder from one tree and one version (electron-builder's `dir` target; the shipped content beside the exe, and `data/` never inside). Shipped art is transcoded PNG → WebP through a content-addressed cache, so a re-run is byte-identical. The version is `package.json`'s alone, and the script refuses a dirty tree. The exe the player plays from is a folder outside the repository kept by `scripts/exeFolder.mjs`: its `data` is its own except `services` and `characters`, which are links to the project's `data`, so the ComfyUI install and the player's characters are shared with the dev app and a character deleted or restored on one side is so on the other. The script never writes inside `data`, never follows a link, refuses while any run is open or when a link stands outside `data`, and its `remove` takes the two links off before deleting anything.
- **Nothing is redistributed**: weights, runtime and nodes are fetched from their own projects at install. `CreditsModal` is the authoritative credit list, headed by the donors and playtesters read off `assets/supporters.json`, a fixed file; a new manifest entry or shipped sound is not done until it has a line in the modal.

## 9. Keeping this guide

**Add**, in the same change:

- a rule or invariant the change relies on that the code cannot say for itself;
- a new mechanic, at one paragraph in section 2;
- a new folder, or a new kind of file, in the map;
- a convention the moment a second file follows it.

**Do not add**:

- anything readable off the code or a comment — schemas, signatures, channel lists, node ids, the words on a screen;
- tuned numbers, rates or thresholds;
- history, rationale essays or rejected alternatives;
- appearance, which is `UI_STYLE_GUIDE.md`'s.

Update a paragraph when a change makes it untrue and delete it when the thing it described is gone. Say each thing once, in whichever of the two guides owns it, and point at the other rather than restating it.
