# Venus University — Design Guide

## 0. How to read this guide

- This is the jumping-off point for anybody, human or agent, changing this repository: what the game is, where things live, and the rules a change has to keep. Read it before the code, and read `UI_STYLE_GUIDE.md` as well for anything that shows on screen.
- The code is authoritative. Where this guide and the code disagree, the code is right and the guide is stale: fix the guide.
- Present tense, nothing aspirational: a feature not in the code does not exist, whatever is written here.
- Appearance — shape, colour, type, layout, motion and the class names that carry them — is `UI_STYLE_GUIDE.md`'s and is never restated here.
- Hand-authored and disk-derived inventories are not listed here: backgrounds, poses, tag vocabularies, the class and shop catalogs, the lorebook, the bots' scripts. Only the rules they follow are.

## 1. What the game is

- A local, single-player, AI-driven dating sim in visual-novel form: an Electron desktop app for Windows with an NVIDIA or an AMD (RDNA 3 or 4) GPU, and a browser build of the same renderer that itch.io hosts. React 19, TypeScript (strict), Zustand, Motion (`motion/react`), Vitest, plain CSS. No server, no accounts, no env vars — everything the player configures lives in `data/settings.json` on the desktop and in the browser's own storage on the web.
- **The writer is a cloud LLM.** By default it is Google Gemini over the native API; the player may instead name any endpoint that speaks OpenAI chat completions, with its own key, model id and reasoning effort. It invents characters, writes every scene as structured JSON the app plays back VN-style, answers two cheap classifiers, and keeps the books between scenes. The persona it writes as is RITA. The cloud pictures — rooms, the graduation picture — are always Gemini's image model, drawn with the Gemini key under either provider and skipped without one.
- **The artist is a locally managed ComfyUI**, installed under `data/services` by the app itself. It is optional: creating characters needs it, playing does not, and in a game it draws only the photos characters text. The browser build has no ComfyUI at all: every control that needs it is dead there under one notice, and the cloud renders — rooms, the graduation picture — still run.
- **The premise is fixed**: the reader is a freshman at Venus University in the city of Veridan for one semester, January 19 (a Monday, day 0) to the Friday nearest May 21. Two slots a day, day and night; a slot holds at most one scene.
- **The end is reachable, not winnable.** Graduation morning opens an authored epilogue of goodbyes. Two things end a playthrough early: debt past a floor, and being caught at something expellable.
- **Content**: scenes may be explicit. Two settings shape it — `noNsfwImages` withholds the nude wardrobe, the CGs and the sexy and nude photos, `lessNsfwText` softens what RITA is asked for. A man has neither the nude wardrobe nor the CGs whatever the setting, while `MEN_ADULT_ART` in `shared/characterRules.ts` is off: never offered, never rendered — the run skips such a bucket whoever asked — and never ready on the stage, so a scene substitutes as the setting does. Neither is a filter, and the notes beside them are the whole of what the app promises.

### Vocabulary that is load-bearing

- **The reader** is the protagonist in the fiction ("he"); **the player** is the person at the keyboard. Prompts and copy say "the reader".
- **Memories** are what one character recalls about the reader. **History** is the playthrough log of finished-scene summaries. The **transcript** is the scene as delivered — the reader's action lines and every reply line — and the writer is shown its tail; the **scene log** is everything the player has read. None of them is "chat history".
- A **playthrough** is one continuous game: a folder of saves plus the one **record** (`playthrough.json`) they are all read against; before the record exists the folder holds one **enrollment** instead, the semester waiting on its timetable. A **save** is a file in it — a slot-save at a boundary, one **autosave**, the last decision point reached, or one of ninety **manual saves** the player writes from the menu.
- An **event** is a plan the reader agreed to with somebody. An **occasion** happens whether he turns up or not, belongs to nobody, and is the only one of the two that can close the university.
- **charId** is the on-disk identity (a UUID). **charKey** (`first_last`) is the prompt identity — the only form the model sees or emits — translated at the edge and never used in a path. Neither is the display name.
- The **roster** is everybody in the playthrough — twelve characters, picked at New Game and never fewer; the **cast** is who is in the scene. A **slot** is one half of one day; the **boundary** is the moment a slot ends and the next begins.
- The **shipped cast** lives under `assets/characters` and is read-only; the **player's own** characters live under `data/characters`.

## 2. Design and mechanics

**Time.** The fixed occasions — holidays, add/drop day, midterms and finals weeks, spring break, summer vacation, graduation — are code. Each save invents a handful more at New Game (a gala, a dance, a career fair, a concert, weekly fillers); a generated occasion can never cancel a class. An occasion announces itself ahead in the prompts — a half-day the slot before, a day the day before, a run as many days before as it lasts — except those with a lead-up of their own. Weekends hold no classes. Spring break sends the half of the roster the reader likes most off campus for the week; summer keeps everybody on campus until graduation.

**Weather.** The whole semester's sky is rolled once at New Game and written on the playthrough record, one reading per slot: clear, rain or a thunderstorm. Rain tends to keep falling for a few half-days, a storm mostly grows out of rain and then clears the air, and storms lean toward the evening and grow commoner as the term runs on; the authored first day, graduation, the Selkie MusicFest and the holidays held outdoors are clear whatever the roll said, and the epilogue's night is always clear. The prompts are told what the sky is doing and for how long it has been doing it. A wet slot draws a background's rain render where one is shipped and the dry one otherwise, plays the rain or storm loop in place of the room tone — heard indoors through a window, so a silent room is not silent under rain, and the landing, where he is indoors on his phone, hears the same window — and wears the weather's mark where the sun or the moon would be: on the splash, the scene stamp, the landing and the calendar, which also forecasts the week ahead.

**Characters.** A character is a man or a woman and is attracted to one of the two, both picked in the New Character modal and changeable in the editor; the reader is always a man. A character is written by the LLM from a short brief and that pick: one woven personality paragraph, five behavior strings (one per relationship stage), a backstory, a four-field love life, likes and dislikes, gift preferences, a closed set of trait switches, the one reader stat she goes for, the pitch of her voice, which the player retunes, booru appearance tags that open on the subject tags every render needs — `1girl` and `mature_female` for a woman, `1boy`, `male_focus`, `mature_male` and `handsome` for a man, swapped when the editor changes the gender — hers to edit like the rest and left out of the writer's prompts, one pose, and a seed. Every image she has comes from ComfyUI under that seed, the photos she texts aside, each under a seed of its own: the expression sprites, and optionally the alternate wardrobes, the CGs and a day/night room. Her portrait is cut from the neutral sprite, which always looks at the viewer whatever eye tag she was written with. A regenerate opens on the prompt it is about to send — every tag as an editable list and the seed it would run under, a new random one by default; one expression is re-rendered on its own by clicking its sprite, as one CG is by clicking it in the gallery. Each of those buttons keeps every group it last sent, whole, on her record and opens on them rather than on her tags from then on, so an edit to her tags reaches it only through the modal's reset, which puts back the groups her record would send and writes nothing until the render goes; the seed is never kept. A text's photo is the exception: its regenerate keeps its groups on the message and writes nothing to her record (The Bunnyboard). A fill, and a button that has sent nothing, render from her tags. Which images exist is read off disk, never recorded, and a partial set counts as absent. Beside the three stock wardrobes she has five custom slots, each a wardrobe rendered from tags the player types and named by him: the slot's Outfit group *is* her record, written before every confirmed render and read back from there when its modal opens, whatever its button kept; a slot is emptied images first and record second, what its button and its sprites' buttons kept going with it; RITA is never offered a custom set and the missing-content sweep never invents one, so the Cast modal's lock is its only way onto the stage. Traits are switches the code branches on and are never written into a prompt. The brief she was written from, reference picture included, sits on her record and beside it in her folder until her sheet lands, so a write that failed or was interrupted is resumed from her card in one click; a card whose sheet was never written opens nothing. The New Character modal's Skip LLM filling box writes her sheet on the spot instead — her names, the description as her personality, the first pose in the manifest her gender may stand in, one stock tag set per expression, `body` as her stat and nothing else — with no brief kept and nothing rendered, so her card opens at once on an empty sheet the player fills and renders by hand. The modal's suggested names and description are written for women, so a man is offered none: a blank field is left to the LLM, and a man with Skip LLM filling waits on both names being typed. A written sheet with an empty field is a failed write, except that a character who cannot be the reader's romance may leave the kinks and the crush and lover behaviors empty. The generation request is written for a woman attracted to men and says otherwise when the character is not one; a man is asked for his appearance and his outfit's skin exposure from his own tag lists, and his draft keeps only a man's categories. The image model leans to women, so every render of a man — sprite, expression and CG — adds a fixed set of women's tags to its negative, at render time and never on the record. A man stands only in the poses the manifest marks for men: the request offers him those alone, a pick outside them falls back to the first of them, and the editor lists them after whatever pose he already has. A man starts at the tallest stage height, anyone else by her breast tag. Everything written about one character — prompts, memories as they are filed, system texts and on-screen labels — is in that character's own pronouns, and every profile the writer is shown opens with the gender outright, since the subject tags never reach it; an instruction about the cast at large, or about a character nobody can name, says "they". Memories already on a save keep the words they were filed in. A character's day carries a mood off a thirty-day menstrual cycle that also keeps her in on her period and makes a Promiscuous girl lustful around ovulation; a man has no cycle, so none of that, and no mood on the cycle's own days. Her pose, like any character's, is picked under Generation settings.

**The slot cycle.** A slot opens on a narration call (the first day is authored) behind a curtain, then the landing: the date, money, stats, the hour's commitments as buttons, topped up with the reader's lowest stats, and a text box. The first action is classified — who is named, class or work or project, public or private, where — the cast is drawn, and the scene call streams lines the player clicks through. Further actions are continuations on a rolling summary until RITA ends the scene. The ending fires its calls in parallel and writes one save when all the replies are in, none when the boundary ahead loses the game; the boundary applies the ledger, moves the clock and writes the slot-save. Everything the ending computes is projected and banked; the boundary assigns and never rolls, so a replayed boundary lands the same values. Once the boundary's curtain is down and before it says anything, the player is asked what each girl in the cast should remember of the scene — the ledger's memories of her, each to reword, retype or blank, and a blank row where it gave her none — and the answer is filed before the clock moves, so the slot-save carries it and a reload before it asks again; a boundary that ends the game, or one with nobody cast, asks nothing. While a reply is pending, still streaming or parked on the scene's ending, the player may interject: the unread lines are deleted, the departure clocks are charged as at any decision point, and an autosave is written before the continuation call goes out. An interjection taken from a rewound line cuts everything after that line, later turns included, and a gift whose turn was cut goes back into the inventory. The row stands over the whole reply, its last line included, until that line is turned; Go from the action box opened over the last line reaches the decision point exactly as a click would, and sends the words as the turn. It is offered nowhere in a solo scene, the orientation scene's first reply, an exam or the ending's status lines, and under a cover; a class scene's first turn locks it instead. A rewind moves back one already-read line, through earlier replies as far as the scene's first reply, never onto the reader's own line, and changes only what is shown; it is closed while a reply's first line has not yet shown; the lines it stepped back may be read forward again, each landing as a cut, the reader's own line passed over without being shown, never into a line not yet read; the decision point is re-reached that way only when the rewind was taken from it. Any reply line of the scene may be rewritten from the chat log — never the reader's own, a status line or the landing's narration before his first action: the rewrite lands on the log, on the transcript line it was delivered as, on the box when it is the line on screen and on the decision point held in memory, so leaving carries it; a rewrite of a line the running summary already covers drops the summary marks past it, so the next call carries the line verbatim; it writes nothing itself. While the scene's ending is under way a line may still be rewritten for as long as the ending can be interrupted, behind a warning Settings can turn off: the ending's banked save and bookkeeping keep the original words, and the summary marks are kept too, dropped only if the player then interjects — so a finished ending files the whole recap and an interjection carries the rewrite verbatim. Interjecting on a reply that ended the scene, or on the goodbye lines after it, warns first and then discards the ending's bookkeeping for the later ending to redo, the texting ledger excepted, which the next ending claims.

**Casting.** The classifier only names; the app decides who is in the room. Every refusal is composed locally — not enrolled, no job, nobody named is free, a name he has not learned. An unnamed public action draws from the free pool, favouring the unmet; a passerby may walk in; whoever is standing at the scene's location joins; the worker on shift is there whether or not named. The cast is fixed for the whole scene. A `hide:` starts a departure clock, and after a grace period off the stage she has left and the model cannot write her back. A CG is offered only while the stage as written holds one girl with a CG set on it, or nobody yet, and it ends the moment anybody else is shown or she herself leaves, by the scene or by the player's hand.

**Relationships are memory, not score.** Nothing about how she feels is a number on disk. Each memory is typed (loved, liked, disliked, hated) and dated; affection is derived from them with a recency weight and bucketed into disposition tiers, which pick the behavior string she is written with. A memory is kept and prompted in the third person, "the reader" every time: the ledgers are asked for it that way, what they return is filed that way, one the player types is filed that way, and every memory the app writes itself is worded that way. Wherever the player reads one it is turned into the second person with the verb agreeing; an older save's second-person memory is shown as it is and turned into the third person for the prompts. From her contact page he may rewrite a memory's verb and words, never its date, and the rewrite reaches every copy she holds. Milestones — met, kissed, slept together, lovers, broke up, friendzoned either way, sharing, contact info, blocked — are flags the ledger's *events* transition, except the two intimate ones, which it reports as acts and the app derives from them; the model is asked for events, never for flags. A crush is rolled once, at the scene ending, gated on the reader meeting her preferred stat. Only a character who can be the reader's romance has any of it: a woman attracted to men, and never a man while `MEN_ROMANCE_READER` in `shared/characterRules.ts` is off — the one switch every romantic mechanic with the reader reads through `romancesReader`. Anybody else never rolls, is told to the writer as no romantic option for him, has every romantic milestone the ledgers report dropped and is taken out of every kiss and night they name, while contact and unblocking still land; romance an older save filed on them is cleared without a word at the next scene, their memories kept. Their preferred stat is what earns their respect instead, said to the writer and in the status hint, and nothing numeric. What her contact page reveals opens by tier and never closes again.

**The reader's stats and money.** Brain, Body and Heart are points on the save, spoken of and shown only as tiers. A stat moves only for something that happened in a scene: what the ledger says the hour exercised (more of it alone than with company), attending class, a grade coming back. Money is not a stat: a dollar balance the ledger prices each scene against, gifts spend and a job pays. It can go negative, and past the debt floor it ends the playthrough. The model is never shown the balance. The reader also has a bio: a few sentences the player writes at New Game (the full flow only) and may rewrite on his profile, carried into every prompt\'s `READER` block as one sentence that completes "The reader is". The save keeps lifetime tallies — money earned, kisses, nights, shifts worked — moved only by the same idempotent boundary passes that move everything else, and beside them the output tokens the writer has generated for this playthrough, thinking included, credited on every cloud text reply as it lands (retries and failed replies too, New Game's own generation calls carried over from the enrollment, and never a picture), so a replayed call is counted again because it was billed again; his GPA is never stored, being derived at read time from the scores frozen and the meetings recorded so far, and standing at 4.0 before either exists.

**Academics.** The player picks lecture classes and a PE class, between a floor and a cap, from a catalog generated for the save; every roster schedule is dealt locally. The generated semester is written to disk the moment the registrar offers it, so a playthrough can be continued from the registrar before its first save exists. Lecture classes are assessed by a midterm and a final built out of the factoids the ledger recorded week by week — a skipped meeting is a question answered wrong. Project classes are assessed at two showcases by sessions worked. A grade is the share his stats buy plus what his work buys of the rest, frozen when the assessment is sat. Add/drop day lets the timetable change once; a drop is permanent.

**Jobs.** The reader may hold one part-time job from a board, on a shift week that can never overlap a class. A shift is worked by spending the slot on it; a missed one is a strike, and enough of them and he is let go; clean streaks raise the wage. What a worked shift pays in stats is the employer's own, rolled when the shift is cast and kept on the scene, so a reload pays the same one. Roster characters hold jobs too, as whereabouts only: she is never judged or paid.

**Whereabouts.** Every character has a hidden weekly schedule of haunts, dealt at New Game after classes and shifts, one person per place per slot. Each haunt comes from one of the profile's menus — somewhere she has fun, somewhere she studies, an activity the profile call describes in her own phrase, a meal out — and beside them the weekly grocery run and the nights she stays in. The kind is stored beside the place: it is what the prompts say she is there for, and what the rule emptying a study haunt through a week nobody has anything to study for reads. A place may keep hours of its own — a club's nights, a restaurant's dinners and weekend brunches — and neither a haunt nor an outing is placed outside them; an employer's shifts are its building's hours. Each slot also rolls an overlay of who is with whom — friends pulled along, weekend outings — off the roster's own affinities: a stored affinity per pair, moved a step by every meeting the slot held and settled at every scene ending. The map shows only what the reader has learned: contacts are told outright, anybody else only on a slot he has already found her on.

**Romance between the roster.** Characters fall for one another off-screen, rolled in the same settle as the pair affinities and banked and applied with them: a crush only on the gender the holder is attracted to, only out of a positive encounter between two friends, one crush and one partner each at a time, declared after a few days on a slot the two spend together — a scene they are both in counts as one, and two in it who both came out of it liking the reader met as friends do — and accepted on a crush returned or a very high affinity; a turned-down declaration costs affinity and keeps the same crush from forming again for a while, a couple fights now and then at the week's end and breaks up once its affinity is negative, and exes never fall for each other again. The reader's lover is taken: she falls for nobody, says no to everybody, and a couple she is in ends, the man of it recorded on the ex as displaced by the reader and given one permanent grudge memory of him, however often or for whomever he is displaced, and a prompt line while it runs for a month; a crush on her is kept to itself while she is his, never declared and so never turned down, and fades unsaid once it has been kept thirty days with no drift standing (the days a drift stood do not count): nothing on record but the same wait a turned-down declaration leaves before it can form again, marked quiet so no prompt tells of it. The couples standing at once are capped. A lover of the reader's who does not share him and is cold to him (affection −15 or lower, kept until it climbs above −10) or was betrayed within three weeks (the rumor pass dates the day she learned of it on her entry) and is not warm to him (affection 0 or lower to open, forgiven above +5), with a crush on her held by somebody who could still declare and be answered (single, no wait after a turn-down, room among the couples, her affinity with him at least the accepting one), starts to drift, and keeps drifting while he stays among her friends: a record keyed by her, with him, the day it began and the days a scene, a thread or its hint post carried its signs, one a day, counted from the slot after it opened. The writer is told of it wherever she or he is, as small signs and nothing decided. Once it has run ten days and shown two signs, still holding, she leaves the reader at that ending's settle, banked and applied at the boundary through the fold a reported breakup takes, its memory giving the reason, and the opening writes the text she ends it with; the drift ends the moment its cause, the crush, his place among her friends or her being his lover stops holding. After that she is single and the declaration follows the usual rules; the game never has her accept anybody while she is his. The writer is told as common knowledge every couple, and every ex pair whose breakup is under a month old or with either of the two present — on the phone, the one texting — and the crushes and recent turn-downs of those present alone, a crush as a secret only its holder knows and shows in small signs; a crush on the reader's lover makes its holder jealous of him, by the prompt alone. A scene is told which of those present are a couple, with a fight of the last week, and which confessions between them the rules allow, and a character's crush and lover behaviors reach it towards a partner or a crush in the room, or an order to work them out from the personality where they are blank. The scene ledger reports a confession and its answer, a breakup and the reader finding out about a crush, and is asked for them only when somebody here is either end of a crush or in a couple, or could fall for somebody else here; each is checked against the same rules — a row the game cannot take is dropped — and applied first in the ending's settle, so it lands at the boundary with the rest; a discovery is kept on the map until the crush it names is gone. The same ledger reports the reader playing wingman, by his words alone: talking one character up to another who is here, which warms the pair and gives a listener drawn to them and free to fall an extra, likelier crush roll, never about or to his lover; badmouthing one to another or keeping them apart, which sours the pair before couples and crushes are judged, so it can end either in the same settle, his lover included; urging somebody here to confess a crush they hold, which marks the crush — its holder then declares at the two's next slot together within a week, whatever its age and odds, once the couples have room — and tells him of it; and telling somebody here of a crush on them, which has the declaration answered in that settle, by the answer the scene reported where the rules allow it and by the usual rule otherwise, as one event with a declaration of the same two the ledger reported. Neither of the last two touches a crush on his lover. Each kind lands once a day per pair; a talk-up and a talk-down of the same pair in one scene cancel out, and one given in a later slot of the same day is dropped. The actions taken are kept on the map for a month. A scene is told of a crush he has found out about as a secret he shares, and, where its holder is away and its object here, how the object would answer if he told. The texting ledger reports none of it. On screen it is read off the map when drawn and never stored beside it: a contact's page says who she is dating and since when, her exes, and a crush of hers he has found out about while she holds it, and lists the days she started dating somebody and broke up beside her posts; the feed carries the same starts and breakups under its friendships' rule, both named and one a contact who has not blocked him. Anybody he cannot name is left out rather than masked, and single is never said. Whoever posts in a slot's opening is told their own love life — a partner and since when, a breakup of the last week, and only that a crush is held, never on whom — with anybody the reader cannot name given as someone not to be named. The ending decides who posts for sure in the slot it opens, off the map before its settle and the one after, and banks it with the opening: one of each couple that formed or ended, a contact who has not blocked him before the other and a pick fixed by the slot between equals, a couple the reader broke up by dating one of them excepted; and for a drift that opened, its lover where he can see her posts and the one she is drawn to otherwise, writing a subtle hint. That hint counts as the drift's sign for the day it goes up, once the boundary has stored the drift and only if it came back written. The ending also picks at most one piece of anonymous gossip for the slot it opens, which that opening writes and the save keeps on the feed for everybody, contact or not: first a lover who left the reader with no number of hers or a block between them, owed a post saying only that she is single until one comes back written; then a drift some days old still short of its signs with none counted that day, the one she is drawn to named only where the reader can name him; then, at small odds each slot, a crush held a while whose two names he knows and he has not found out about. The writer is given the names it may use and nothing of the reader, and a post that names somebody he cannot name, as a whole word in any case, is dropped as if it never came. What the post counts for — a drift's sign dated that day and one a day at most, the crush found out, the lover no longer owed — counts once the boundary has stored the map and only for a post that came back. The map keeps no half of the day, so such a row is ordered as the latest half of its date not yet to come. Nothing in it moves his standing with anybody but a lover who leaves him, the man he displaced and whoever his wingman actions touched. Those are remembered of him in the settle, banked and filed at the boundary on the same list the displaced man's grudge is kept on, which reaches every prompt and her contact page like any memory, is never asked about at the boundary, and files each reason once, a repeat taking the new date: a couple formed out of a declaration while his latest action on the pair in a couple of weeks before was help rather than badmouthing leaves the one who declared loving it and the one who said yes liking it; a declaration he told of that is turned down leaves its maker hating it, and one he urged that is turned down with the mark still standing disliking it, the telling alone counting where both apply; and a talk-down whose victim was away from the scene reaches the victim, as hatred, when anybody else in it stood among the victim's friends before the talk moved the pair, at most once a day per victim and told on to nobody, as the one memory that names nobody who heard it, so each found out refreshes it, while one who was there is left to the ledger. None of these is filed for somebody who was in the scene when the scene itself showed the outcome — a declaration the ledger reported, or the answer it reported to a crush he told of — since the ledger remembers that for them. The development build alone shows the rest, in a read-out F9 opens over a game with the romance the last scene call carried, the wingman actions kept and queued and the latest events applied, from a scene or from the rolls, with each memory his wingman actions filed or skipped and each badmouthing that went unnoticed, and why, held in memory only, and, under it, buttons that set a game up to see it: a crush, a couple — which the next ending reads as news against the map from before the first one, held in memory and dropped when another game is entered — an affinity, an affection, the reader's lover, a betrayal, a drift's age, a kept crush's age, a crush found out, a crush leaking in the next ending's gossip — held in memory until the boundary — a block, an encouragement's age, the age of the wingman actions naming somebody, the age of a breakup, and each wingman action and two characters spending the slot together, queued for the ending of the scene under way, read there beside the ledger's own and dropped at the boundary, each written the way the game writes it and refused where the game's rules would; `npm run sim:romance` plays the roster's semester through the game's own encounter rolls to report what it produced. A save without the map or the gossip, a couple without a fight date, a crush without an encouragement, a talk-down without its victim finding out, an opening without wingman memories or a map without discoveries, drifts or wingman actions reads as none, and an ex without a displaced man is no rival.

**Rumors.** The ledger reports every kiss and every night as an *act*: who was in it — two or more only when they were all in it at once, which makes each of them a sharer — and whether anybody not taking part could have seen it. A girl in an act the reader is not dating is its subject; a lover is nobody's business. A public act is seen by everybody else in the room who was not in it, or, with nobody there, by one girl with nowhere to be; a private one leaves those same girls only a sign. What a girl holds about the reader and another girl is one dated suspicion, refreshed by every sign she gets and lapsing after the last of them. A girl who watched it, or who gets a second sign about the same girl while the first still stands, tells her friends and whoever she spent the slot with, once; being told is itself a sign. Lovers, crushes and the friends of a lover he has just cheated on take it personally the moment they see or hear it, as a permanent memory worded by which of the two it was; a girl who has agreed to share minds nothing, and nobody in the act ever witnesses it. None of it is read out at the scene's end: he learns what she saw or heard the next time he meets or texts her, since her memories reach every prompt. Dating is public: the reader's own block says who he is with and since when, an open relationship as such, and his exes with the dates theirs ran between. Starting with somebody new while a lover who has not agreed to share is still his leaves her that moment — she is left without a word said to him, she remembers being left for the other girl, and the opening writes the texts she ends it with — while a new lover beside one who shares is sharing too, and the girls with a crush of their own and the friends of the girl he left mind it as well; the milestone screens report only what the scene itself reached, never the dating pass. BunnyBot texts once the first time anybody sees him with a girl, and once with the two-timing tip.

Where a thing goes:

## 4. Architecture rules

- **The renderer touches nothing but the bridge.** On the desktop, main is behind it and is the only process that touches disk, spawns a process or calls a network; in the browser build, `src/web` implements the same bridge over IndexedDB and `fetch`, and beyond `platform` the renderer cannot tell which it is talking to.
- **The window shows one page and opens no other.** A navigation the renderer starts is refused; a link leaves through main and only for an `https:` page or a `mailto:` address; the renderer is sandboxed, with devtools in dev only. In the browser build a wheel over the stage never scrolls the page the game is embedded in unless a scroller under the pointer can still take it.
- **Every channel is `invoke`/`handle` and answers `Result<T>`** — `{ ok, data }` or `{ ok: false, error }` — with errors built only by `appError`. Nothing throws across the bridge and nothing is swallowed. Adding a channel edits `preload/api.d.ts`, `preload/index.ts` and `main/ipc.ts`.
- **Progress rides fixed broadcast channels**, never per-job channel names: the renderer learns a job id only after the events have fired.
- **Character images reach the screen through one URL resolver — `charimg://` on the desktop, a blob URL in the browser — and backgrounds through the bundler.** The URL always names the PNG; the shipped cast is the same image under `.webp`, every reader accepts either extension and every writer makes a PNG. Which images a character has is read off her folder on the desktop and off the pack index or the database keys in the browser, never recorded. A URL can only show an image; what needs pixels — the wardrobe repairs, the hand fix, the graduation sheet — reads bytes over the bridge.
- **Sound is bytes over IPC into one Web Audio graph**, with one bus per slider. What plays continuously is derived from store state by one pure function, and what just happened by a second, with a reloaded save counting as nothing having happened. A missing file warns once and is silence.
- **The job queue is serial and global.** Failures retry with backoff only for transport errors; there is exactly one permanent-vs-retryable list, in `shared/errors.ts`, and both processes read it. `cancelGroup` resolves only once the running job has settled; loop calls register under three cancel groups — the scene calls, the ending's bookkeeping, and everything else — and leaving cancels all three, an interjection the first, an ending interrupt the first two. No wait inside a job is unbounded; every continuation re-checks that its character is still wanted after each `await`.
- **Nothing in the UI waits for ComfyUI**: callers fire and forget or queue a job, and the first job pays the cold start. Main owns the server's runtime state and broadcasts it on one fixed channel; the renderer's store mirrors it and never infers it from a call's result. ComfyUI starts when the player asks for it in Manage Characters, after an install completes and when a render job needs it, and nowhere else — never at boot, and a game only through the first photo a character texts, never by being loaded. Manage Characters can stop it by hand, and a stopped server stays down until the player starts it or a render job does.
- **One store per domain, no god store.** Only stores talk to the bridge; components read state and call actions; state one screen alone needs stays in React.
- **The game loop is imperative module code**, `gameLoop.ts` calling `stores/loop/*` with imports running one way and back-edges late-bound through `loop/hooks.ts`; there is no import cycle in `stores/`. Everything held between calls is one `loopState` object; `runToken` identifies one stay in a game and is re-checked after every `await`. At most one live scene call is ever in flight. The flags the screen must watch — a resolved scene's pending ending, whether an ending's bookkeeping is still out, the status sequence, the boundary's memory question — live on the store beside `busy`/`streaming`.
- **Errors have three tiers and none is silent**: a job failure marks its card and offers retry; a recoverable app error is a modal the app continues past; a fatal one is a blocking screen with copyable diagnostics.
- **Every console line lands in `data/app.log`** — the browser build keeps the same capped log in its database — from both processes: main writes its own as it prints, the renderer's are forwarded over one channel. A log that will not open costs nothing but the log. The Feedback modal hands the player a copy of it to send in, so nothing may print a secret. Both sinks — the file, and the error a failed channel answers with — replace the install, data and home paths with placeholders, so no line and no modal names the machine.
- **On disk, and in the browser's database**, every JSON record carries a `schemaVersion`; a read refuses a wrong version or a missing required field and defaults nothing. The one exception is a text's photo: a field that does not read whole, the groups it keeps included, is shown as no photo and never redrawn, the save still loads, and the field is written back as it was found. The second is the photo a banked opening's invitation carries: one that does not read whole, or is of a kind no invitation carries, is no photo, and its invitation is filed without it. Two records carry an upgrade, applied on read by a pure function beside the read rule and written straight back where it was read from: the character record, from either of the two versions before this build's, a step at a time, with `updatedAt` untouched — the last step reads `1boy` in the appearance as a man, whose appearance then leads with `1boy` and `male_focus` and drops a woman's subject tags, anyone else as a woman, each attracted to the other gender, and an export's manifest from either version is read as this build's; and the settings, whose custom endpoint's model ids move out of Gemini's fields when the file names none of the endpoint's own, which a file this build wrote always does. Every other record, and every other version, is refused, with no migration path. Writes are atomic: a temp file renamed over the target, the rename retried for about a second when Windows refuses it because a scanner or the indexer holds the file it just saw change. A write that is several steps — a slot save pruning the window, a staged set committing — is one transaction in the browser. Any id that becomes a path segment is validated first. An absent optional is omitted, never `null`. What New Game settles goes on the playthrough record, written once and replacing the enrollment it was picked on; what moves goes on the save. A save may carry its own picture inside it, so a backup and a restore carry it too.
- **The reader's picture is the one file under a playthrough the player supplies**; the photos characters text are files the game writes there itself (below). The renderer cuts it to the portrait's proportions and encodes a PNG; whichever build writes it sniffs the bytes first under one shared rule; it is kept beside the record like the graduation picture, goes with the playthrough when that is deleted, and travels in a backup, where the export sniffs it again.
- **A text's photo is a file under its playthrough's `photos` folder named for its message, its seed and a code of its prompts** — one file per version, so a save reaches the version it records whatever was drawn since — the id and the seed validated before they become a path and the code hashed off the prompts by one shared function, in main off the prompts it is sent; a reader takes only that exact name, and a name of another shape is never read. Its job carries the playthrough's id, so it lands in the right folder whichever game is open: it draws into `data/tmp` and moves the picture into place only while it is still wanted, the playthrough's folder is still there — never made again — and the bytes are a PNG, the temporary file going whatever fails. A photo asked for again waits on the job already queued. Deleting a playthrough cancels its photos and waits for them first; a restore does the same for every photo. Photos never travel in a backup: the message keeps the seed and the prompts, and an opened conversation redraws whatever file is missing. A screen holds a photo's state by playthrough, message and version, shows a job's result only while the playthrough it was asked for is still open, and drops every state and picture on the way out of a game.
- **A save is written only at a point the player can be put back onto**, and every reply that arrived is paid for by a write before playback resumes. The registrar is such a point before any save exists, which is what the enrollment is for. Leaving for the menu and quitting the app from the Escape menu both write the decision point first. The autosave is the last decision point reached and stands until the next one overwrites it; a boundary leaves it alone. An ending the boundary will lose to — the ledger's expulsion, or the balance past the floor once the scene's money has moved — is written nowhere: no autosave, and the manual save is refused over its goodbye and status lines, so a lost game leaves only the saves before it. An interjection writes the cut scene as a decision point before its call goes out; a rewind writes nothing. A scene ending is one write or none. A manual save is refused while a call it would lose is out — a reply, a classifier, an ending's bookkeeping, a slot opening, a text or its typed release, a failed turn parked on retry — and it is absent from the goodbye menu once the balance is past the debt floor. Where it can be taken, a manual save records the line on screen with the lines not yet read still queued, so a load lands on that line and reads on with no call; while the ending's status lines are on screen it records the ending's start, taken before any money moved, so a load replays the sequence and pays once; mid-paper it records the question the reader is on. The autosave for an exam is written once, when its questions land, and a reload sits the paper again from its first question; a manual save resumes at its own question instead. A manual save and the autosave carry a small picture of the stage a load of them opens on — for a reply still queued, as its first spoken line leaves it — drawn from the same images the stage shows and only when somebody is on it; the load list's background for a save without one is read off the same lines; a boundary save carries none, and the load list shows the landing's background for it. Every pass a boundary runs is idempotent, because the boundary is replayed whenever the save before it is loaded.
- A queued save write belongs to the stay that queued it and is dropped if that stay has been left; leaving, quitting and switching saves wait for every queued write.
- The load list is read as summaries; a save is read whole only when it is loaded, and the newest save of a playthrough, whichever kind, is what Continue opens and what the playthrough list shows.
- **What a screen shows is derived where it can be**: the displayed stage, the calendar's classes, the map's rows and the feed's check-ins are computed at read time from facts, never stored beside them.
- **Booru tags are lowercase with underscores** in every vocabulary and prompt, normalized at the source; weight syntax is preserved as written; nothing the model returns is re-checked against a vocabulary except the pose, which cannot render without a skeleton and which a man may take only from the poses marked for men.
- **The custom slots are outfit sets like any other.** Their ids are appended to the outfit vocabulary, so every gate, folder, URL, render, repair and lock takes them as it takes the stock sets; only what labels a set, reads its tags, offers it to RITA or sweeps for it tells the two apart. A record carries a seed flag only for a set it has rendered, and a set with no flag follows the main seed.
- **A character's record has one writer in the renderer**, serialized per character: every write reads her fresh at its turn, so a seed a bucket records and a name the player types cannot lose each other, and a Save from the editor keeps the fields the run owns.
- **Configuration is the settings file**, edited through the app. Settings writes a select, a checkbox or a slider the moment it changes; the typed fields — the endpoint URL, the model ids, the reply cap and the keys — are staged behind Save and gated on the way out. The two keys — the Gemini key, which writes under Gemini and draws the cloud pictures under either provider, and a custom endpoint's own key beside it — are encrypted at rest and never reach the renderer, which sees a presence flag for each. Switching providers moves neither key and changes no other setting: each provider keeps its own model, secondary model and effort, so one switched away and back finds them as they were; a custom endpoint's key is kept only for the origin it was typed for, so a key never follows the player to another host. A custom endpoint's URL is any well-formed `http:` or `https:` URL, saved as typed, the Test connection button being how the player learns whether it answers; a provider switched to Custom before its URL is typed is kept off a game by the writer-ready gate. The browser build is served over https, so a browser lets it reach `http:` only on this machine. The browser build holds the keys for the session only unless the player opts to keep them in the browser's storage — an origin every other itch.io game shares, and one the browser can clear. Either build writes a backup of everything it keeps — one zip in one format, never a key — and restores one behind a confirmation: saves, settings and grab bags are replaced and the player's own characters merged in by id, once every image in the archive has passed the same content check an import does. The shipped cast is the build's: it never travels in a backup, and a backup that names one of its ids is restored around her, record and images alike, so a change to the cast in one build is not written over by a backup made in another. A few dev switches are hand-edited and have no UI; the browser build fixes them at their defaults.
- **The app comes up behind a cover already down.** Nothing is drawn until the stores are in and the first screen has been chosen under it; a fatal error drops the cover with the sequence it was raised for.
- **The first run is a sequence of stages on one screen** — the API key, the ComfyUI install, the content question — where a stage already settled is skipped and a player who quits part-way is put back at the first stage still unsettled. An update offer, where there is one, comes before the first stage. The key stage offers both providers — a Gemini key and model, or a custom endpoint's URL, model id and optional key with a Test connection — stages everything locally and writes nothing until Save; a custom endpoint's effort, reply cap and secondary model wait in Settings. The content question is last, and answering it is what ends the run. The browser build has no install stage, and a returning visitor who did not keep the key is put on the key stage alone.
- **Authored pools are dealt from grab bags**: the stranger's post, the inspiration word, the rumor place, the filler sentences. Half the pool drains before anything can repeat. The set-aside lists live in `data/grabbags.json`, shared by every playthrough, written behind the draw and flushed before the app quits. The stranger post's handle bag holds the generated names beside every supporter's, a supporter carrying as many marbles as their contribution earns on a diminishing scale, and the handle is drawn before the message: a marble cannot repeat before the bag turns, but a name with several of them can, and a line marked strangers-only is never dealt to a supporter's name. The gossip's handle comes from a bag of its own that holds the generated names alone.

## 5. Prompts and the LLM

- **Builders are pure** and return `{ system, user, schema, cacheKey }`; main attaches the key and sends. Arrays are joined with newlines, so one element is one line the model reads — no sentence is ever broken across two. A builder never draws from a grab bag: the caller draws the inspiration word or the rumor place and passes it in.
- **Order is by rate of change, slowest first.** `system` is what never varies while a playthrough runs (persona, JSON rules, setting); `user` is everything else, cheapest-to-change last and the reader's action at the very end. Scene calls key their cache on the playthrough, never the save; one-shots key on a constant. A list that reaches a prompt is sorted, never shuffled, so the cached prefix holds. A `user` message that opens with a preamble fixed for its cacheKey marks where the preamble ends with `logFrom`, and the console log starts there; the system prompt is never logged.
- **The writer's window is the running summary plus the transcript's tail.** Every continuation and closing call sends the summary alongside the latest stretch of the transcript verbatim, taken as whole lines from the tail under a word budget and never shorter than the part the summary does not yet cover; the summary a reply arrives with covers the transcript up to the point that reply's prompt was built, and each summary is kept with the mark it covers up to, so a cut reverts to the right one. The ledger reads the whole scene.
- **Structured outputs everywhere**: the schema goes verbatim as `responseJsonSchema` to Gemini and as a non-strict `response_format` JSON schema to a custom endpoint; declared key order is load-bearing (`lines` before `summary`), and leaving a property out of the schema is the only reliable way to stop the model filling it. Gemini rejects a schema past a complexity cap with a bare `INVALID_ARGUMENT`, so a reply carrying a record per character is an array of records with a `key` enum and no item bounds.
- **Never trust a reply.** Every value is sanitized: an unknown key, background or action is dropped or defaulted with a console warning, never thrown on, because one bad field must not cost the player the scene. Streaming is a preview channel only; the resolved reply is sanitized whole and reconciled, and a preview that disagrees is discarded rather than spliced. The one carve-out is an interjection: lines the player has already read from a call he then interjects over are canon, and the call is aborted.
- **Prefetch, bank, apply.** The next slot's opening is fetched at the scene ending and banked on the save, the photo one of its invitations carries and the likes of the reader's waiting posts settled and banked with it; a hangout scene is written the moment it is armed; the texting ledger is sent when the scene's cast is set and its reply banked on the scene, keyed on the message set it read, so a reload claims it rather than paying again; an exam's chain when its questions land. An interrupted ending's banked and in-flight bookkeeping is dropped and re-sent by the later ending, except the texting ledger, which the next ending claims. Nothing an ending reads is applied, only projected; the boundary applies.
- **A request is built once and re-sent verbatim.** A retryable code spends a silent backoff budget first, except a depleted prepaid balance, which a resend cannot refill; a content block earns one silent shortened resend; a malformed reply is never auto-retried once the player has read its preview. What survives is a modal, and leaving it loses nothing, because nothing was written; editing the prompt by hand is offered only on a content block, since nothing else that failed was the prompt.
- **Four kinds of call may route to a cheaper secondary model** — the slot opening, which the epilogue's status updates ride, texting, the exam quiz and the solo scene; cast scenes, ledgers, classifiers and character generation never do. Both classifiers run at no less than low thinking and both ledgers at high, whatever the setting says. A custom endpoint's effort is minimal until set, and the floors raise it like Gemini's.
- **Ask for events, not flags; for a boolean, not a magnitude.** The app holds every flag and applies every transition. The model is never shown the balance, never told the departure grace period, and never given a character's haunts.
- **The ledgers are asked for memories in the reader's voice** — "the reader", never "you" — and every desc is put through the same rewrite again as it is injected, so an older save's second-person memory reaches the model the same way.
- **The classifier decides nothing.** It names, the app enforces, and every refusal is local; a transport failure parks the turn rather than degrading to a guess.
- **Every scene-kind fact is decided where the verdict is cast**, held on the store, persisted on the scene, and injected on every turn of it.
- **The lorebook is keyword-triggered** on the turn's own words and on the place the classifier named; entries never cascade. An uncast character who is named gets a paragraph that says she is absent, what she looks like, and where she stands with the cast and the reader.
- **Authored text never meets the LLM or the sanitizer**: the first day, the bots, the game-over endings, the graduation scroll and every status line are the app's own writing.

## 6. Coding conventions

- TypeScript strict with `noImplicitAny` on explicitly. There is no linter; `npm run typecheck` and `npm test` are the automated checks.
- **Naming**: PascalCase only for React component and view files; camelCase for everything else. CSS classes are `vu-` prefixed and every visual rule lives in `vu_styles`.
- **Imports**: `@shared` is the only alias and is used for every cross-tree import of `src/shared`; imports inside a tree stay relative.
- **Comments say what the thing below them is or does.** One above every function, one or two lines, three where a mechanic genuinely needs it; a file's docblock at most five. Never what it isn't, never a rejected alternative, never history, and never a pointer to a guide or a section. A rule that needs explaining belongs in this guide, once.
- **Pure and impure halves are separate**: a roll takes `rand` as an argument, the impure edge reads and writes the store, and a test reaches the pure half.
- **A vocabulary is authoritative in code** and printed into the prompt and the schema from the same constant, so the two cannot drift; a new value is appended, never inserted, so cached prefixes stay byte-identical.
- **One definition per fact.** When two places must agree — how a charKey is derived, whether she is busy, whether a code is permanent, whether a schedule is complete, who is in a class — they call one function.
- **A control that is not the player's to use is absent; one that is dead says nothing.** A disable notice is an exception approved by name. Placeholders are real values a blank field submits, with one exception: an empty prose field of the character sheet shows the shape its text wants, told about her by name, and submits nothing.
- **A character whose name the reader has not learned is never shown by face or name** outside the scene she is in: showing the face is naming her, and a stranger is omitted rather than masked.
- **Nothing is shown as points.** A number on screen is a tier; the raw figure appears in one place, small, beside the chart.

## 7. Tests

- The suite defends **logic that can silently corrupt a save, and the pure functions a save is computed from**. A loud failure is found the first time anyone plays; a plausible-but-wrong save is invisible for hours. IPC wiring, the ComfyUI and setup services, and every view stay untested by design.
- **Never test** prompt wording or block order, formatting, labels or copy, or shipped content — a lint of a hand-written file restates the file.
- **Err on the side of fewer tests.** Creating none for a feature and verifying it by hand is a valid outcome. Anything neither the type checker nor the suite can catch is exercised in the running app before it is called done.
- The environment is `node`: no test touches the DOM or React; the browser database is exercised under a fake in that same environment. Stores are exercised through `setState`/`getState`. `test/fixtures.ts` holds every builder more than one file wants, and `stubApi`, which installs a partial `window.api` — anything not stubbed throws. A builder that reads a store stays in its own file: `fixtures.ts` imports no store, or a suite that mocks a module beneath one would load that store from inside its own mock factory.
- One file per unit under test; the file names are the index, and each `describe` says what it defends.

## 8. External services

- **ComfyUI** is installed, verified and run by the app under `data/services/comfyui`: a runtime release, its custom nodes and its models, with `shared/setupManifest.ts` authoritative for every tag, commit, URL, size and hash. The runtime is one of two pinned assets of that release, NVIDIA or AMD ROCm, chosen by the GPU vendors Electron reports and overridden by the hand-edited `comfyGpu`; both are launched with the same arguments, ComfyUI reading its backend off the torch it was built against, and a build that is not the one the machine resolves to fails verification rather than falling back to the CPU, the next install replacing it and everything under it. On the ROCm build alone the background-removal node's source is patched to run its model on the CPU, at install and again at every boot that finds the patch missing; the NVIDIA build's file is never touched. Nothing is fetched from a moving reference — the runtime comes from a named release tag and a node from a named commit — and no download is accepted that is not over https or does not match the manifest's size and SHA256; a node's Python dependencies come from the package index only, at the exact versions the manifest names, since a player has neither git nor a build chain. Every zip the app opens is unpacked by the bundled 7-Zip and only after its whole listing has been read and accepted: no entry may be absolute, leave the destination, repeat a name or be a link, and an archive past its entry-count or uncompressed-size cap is refused unopened. A character import and a backup restore hold to the same rule once the zip is open: besides the record, the marker and a staged run's images, every entry must be one of her images under either extension with its bytes sniffed rather than trusted, or the whole archive is refused before anything is written, and the export, duplicate and backup writers copy only what that gate accepts, so the app never writes an archive it would refuse. The workflows are API-format graphs executed as they are, every prompt string injected per job. One seed per character, the photos characters text aside; a regenerate renders into staging and commits by a directory swap, a fill writes in place. A single expression and a single CG render in place under a seed nobody records; a single expression is a face pass over the set's base frame, which is rendered only where the set has none. The app trusts only the server it owns: one already answering on the port is adopted only while a process is running from the app's own interpreter, an output path the server names is used only where it lands inside the output folder, and an image it answers is written only when its first bytes are a PNG's or a JPEG's. The development build's Manage Characters alone opens a photo lab, whose channels a packaged build refuses: it draws a portrait photo of any woman on the shelf, the shipped cast included, from tag groups it builds off her record, a kind, an outfit, a place, the half of the day and the weather, and shows for the player to rewrite; a box, ticked by default, sends it through the refining graph every photo in a game is drawn with; each render is queued under a group of its own and lands, with a text file of its prompts and seed beside it, in a folder under `data/tmp` that only the lab's own button empties, never in her folder.
- **The cloud LLM** is called by one adapter per wire format: Gemini's native `generateContent`, and OpenAI chat completions for the custom endpoint. The transport, the vendor table and the adapters are three files with three jobs; the table alone names a vendor for writing, and the custom entry's root is read off the settings at call time. The pictures always run on the Gemini adapter with the Gemini key, which the picture transport names outright, and the Settings form's two probes — the endpoint's model list and a one-line test call — go through the same transport. Every text call is capped at one shared output-token ceiling, whichever adapter sends it, except that a custom endpoint sends the player's own cap where one is set. What every text reply generated, thinking included, is read off the vendor's usage block by the transport on every exit — a blocked, truncated or malformed reply included — and reported through a port each process wires, reaching the renderer on one fixed broadcast channel before the reply itself does; the pictures report nothing. A cloud image is kept exactly as the model answered it, JPEG or PNG, under its `.png` name; the night re-light is the one reader that needs the type and sniffs it off the bytes.
- **Updating.** The desktop asks itch.io's channel endpoint for the Windows build's user-version at every boot, offline answered with silence, and compares it to its own. A newer one is offered once per launch before any screen, the first run's stages included, unless the Settings checkbox has turned that offer off; either way the main menu carries a notice that offers it again, and an update taken from there runs under a curtain that lifts back onto the menu if it fails. On Update the build is fetched through the store page's own download handshake, unpacked under `data/update` — once whatever the last update left there has been swept — behind the same zip gate as every archive, and checked to be a newer build of this app by its manifest before anything moves. The swap runs after the app has quit, by the app's own exe in Node mode on a helper script it copied out, by renames only with a journal and rollback, and the app relaunches itself; `data/` is never touched. A swap that did not land leaves a note in `data/update`, which the next boot reads before sweeping the folder and says in a modal before anything else is asked. A file is removed only when the installed build's manifest listed it and the new build's does not, so an install without a manifest gets every file replaced and nothing removed. `resources/build-manifest.json` is written at packaging, once the exe's icon and version strings are in, and is what both sides are planned from. `updateAsVersion` and `updateFeed` are hand-edited dev switches.
- **Release.** `npm run release` builds both artifacts from one tree and one version: the browser folder and its zip (`index.html` at the root, every URL relative, asserted against itch.io's caps before it is zipped) and the Windows app folder and its zip (electron-builder's `zip` target, never `portable`; the shipped content beside the exe, and `data/` never inside; the folder is refused unless every file in it, and nothing else, is in its manifest at the size and hash it has). Shipped art is transcoded PNG → WebP through a content-addressed cache, so a re-run is byte-identical and butler's patches stay small; the desktop ships the cast as files, the browser as one stored zip per character in the export format. The version is `package.json`'s alone: `npm version` bumps and tags it, and the script refuses a dirty tree and a tag that points elsewhere. The push's `--userversion` is what the updater's check reads, so a build pushed without one is invisible to it.
- **Nothing is redistributed**: weights, runtime and nodes are fetched from their own projects at install. `CreditsModal` is the authoritative credit list, headed by the donors and playtesters read off `assets/supporters.json`, which `npm run supporters` derives from the ledger in `private/`; a new manifest entry or shipped sound is not done until it has a line there, and a new supporter is not done until the script has run and the derived file is committed.
- **Public mirror.** `scripts/syncPublic.mjs`, driven by `.github/workflows/sync-public.yml`, publishes a snapshot of this repository's `main` to https://github.com/venus-uni-dev/venus-university on every push, one squashed commit per push. `isPublic` in that script is the only rule deciding what goes public; a new folder under `assets/` is private until it is added to its allowlist there. `assets/characters` reaches the mirror as one stored zip per character, built from HEAD's blobs with a fixed entry date so an unchanged cast produces no commit, and the sync fails on a zip of 100 MiB or more, GitHub's file cap. `.github/`, `build/itch-page/`, `private/` and the two music folders `assets/sound/music` and `assets/sound/ambient_music` never ship; `assets/sound/README.md` lists where each excluded track comes from. The runner fetches the mirror with a blob filter, so the public art is never downloaded. A commit that touches the ledger is titled without a figure, since the mirror's squashed commit carries every private subject. The mirror is never merged from. The mirror is now live.

## 9. Keeping this guide

**Add**, in the same change:

- a rule or invariant the change relies on that the code cannot say for itself;
- a new mechanic, at one paragraph in section 2;
- a new folder, or a new kind of file, in the map;
- a convention the moment a second file follows it.

**Do not add**:

- anything readable off the code or a comment — schemas, signatures, channel lists, node ids, the words on a screen;
- tuned numbers, rates or thresholds;
- history, rationale essays or rejected alternatives;
- appearance, which is `UI_STYLE_GUIDE.md`'s.

Update a paragraph when a change makes it untrue and delete it when the thing it described is gone. Say each thing once, in whichever of the two guides owns it, and point at the other rather than restating it.
